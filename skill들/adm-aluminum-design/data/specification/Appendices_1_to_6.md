# Appendix 4 Design for Fire Conditions

This appendix provides provisions for the evaluation of aluminum structural members and frames subjected to fire, determining heat input, thermal expansion, and reduction in strength due to elevated temperatures.

## 4.1 GENERAL PROVISIONS

Design for fire conditions shall comply with the engineering standards and methods of analysis set forth in Sections 4.2 or 4.3 or the requirements for design by qualification testing provided in Section 4.3. The method of demonstrating compliance for a building under fire conditions shall include criteria related to building fire, and provide evidence of compliance with the requirements of this Specification in a manner that permits fire resistance of aluminum structures subject to the demands of specified aluminum structures.

### 4.1.1 Definitions:

The provisions in this section use the following terms as defined below:

*design-basis fire:* a fire having a heat release rate as activated by a fire to mitigate adverse effects or rarely to protect the structure. Heat release rate is generally limited to the combustion of the fuel.

*elements that have a specific fire endurance:* members, structural floors, ceilings, or other elements covered below (e.g., in a structure).

*thermal expansion:* temperature as it occurs of the anticipated fire, as determined in accordance with Section 4.2.1. *Fire* shall provide the elevated temperature required to determine the thermal expansion rate. The material shall be assumed to expand uniformly with thermal expansion rate determined per Section 4.2.3.2.

*fire destructive heating:* as manifested by any or all of the following conditions: distortion, deformation of an element; burning residual materials and moved in accordance with ASTM E119 or other fire test or evidence test to determine the fire resistance.

*fire resistance rating:* the time period of fire resistance obtained by a prescribed testing method until a specified condition is reached in an assembly or determined to exhibit the conditions of a material in assembly controlled within the fire.

The provisions also allow for:

a) Determination of the heat release rate as a function of time or extends the principle of excessive heat, fuel gases, or flames through the building.

*heating:* the transition to a state of rapid surface involvement over a fire of combustible materials within an enclosure.

*heat release rate:* the rate at which thermal energy is generated by a burning material.

An individual is considered in building where the surrounding or supporting structure is capable of resisting substantial heat release.

thermal expansion throughout the range of anticipated elevated temperatures.

## 4.2 STRENGTH AND REQUIRED STRENGTH

The required strength of the structure and its elements shall be determined using heat and resistance factor design (LRFD) or allowable strength design (ASD).

The required strength of the structure shall be calculated by the principle of excessive heat and gases created by building under fire ignition. Values of heat release rate shall be determined using the heat release time curve as the design-basis fire. The design of the structure for fire conditions shall be determined using heat transfer analysis. Fire conditions and heat transfer analysis shall be determined using the principles of this section.

### 4.2.1 Design-basis Fires

#### 4.2.1.1 Localized Fire

Where the heat release rate from the fire is insufficient to cause flashover, a localized fire exposure shall be assumed. In such cases, the fuel composition, arrangement of the fuel, and area occupied by the fuel shall be used to determine the radiant heat flux from the flame and smoke plume to the structure.

#### 4.2.1.2 Post-Flashover Compartment Fires

Where the heat release rate from the fire is sufficient to cause flashover, a post-flashover compartment fire shall be assumed. The determination of the temperature versus time profile resulting from the fire shall include design heat load, ventilation characteristics of the space (natural and mechanical), compartment dimensions, and thermal characteristics of the compartment boundary.

#### 4.2.1.3 Exterior Fires

The exposure of exterior structure to flames projecting from wall openings as a result of a post-flashover compartment fire shall be considered along with the radiation from the interior fire through the opening. The size and length of the flame projection and distance between the flame and the exterior structure shall be used to determine the heat flux to the aluminum. The method in Section 4.2.1.2 shall be used to define the interior compartment fire characteristics.

#### 4.2.1.4 Fire Duration

The fire duration in a particular area shall be determined by considering the total combustible mass, the available fuel in the space. In the case of a localized fire or a post-flashover fire, the time duration shall be determined as the total combustible mass divided by the mass loss rate, except where determined from Section 4.2.1.2.

#### 4.2.1.5 Active Fire Protection Systems

The effects of active fire protection systems shall be considered when determining the design-basis fire.

Where automatic smoke and heat vents are installed in non-sprinklered spaces, the resulting smoke temperature shall be determined from calculation.

### 4.2.2 Temperatures in Structural Systems under Fire Conditions

Temperatures within structural members, components, and frames due to heating conditions used by the design-basis fire shall be determined by a heat transfer analysis.

### 4.2.3 Material Properties at Elevated Temperatures

#### 4.2.3.1 Mechanical Properties

The deterioration in strength and stiffness of structural members shall be accounted for in the structural analysis. The modulus of elasticity at elevated temperatures $E_{eo}$ shall be determined from test data or Table 4.1. Yield strengths $F_{cy}$ and ultimate strengths $F_{tuo}$ at elevated temperatures shall be determined from test data or Table 4.2.

#### 4.2.3.2 Thermal Expansion

Thermal expansion for temperatures between 70°F and 600°F (20°C and 320°C) shall be determined using an expansion coefficient for aluminum of 13.2 × 10⁻⁶ in/in/°F (23.7 × 10⁻⁶ m/m/°C).

#### 4.2.3.3 Specific Heat

The specific heat of aluminum alloys is 0.23 Btu/(lb·°F) (963 J/(kg·°C)).

### 4.2.4 Structural Design Requirements

#### 4.2.4.1 General Requirements

Structures shall provide adequate strength and deformation limits under fire exposure. The structural system shall be designed to maintain stability of the structure and sufficient deflections during the design-basis fire.

#### 4.2.4.2 Thermal Expansion

Individual members shall be provided with adequate clearance to permit thermal expansion, and removable connections shall develop the strength of the connected elements during the design-basis fire.

Connections shall develop the strength of the connected elements. When the stresses due to thermal expansion, clearance and connections exceeds the deformation limits, alternative methods shall be used to address these effects during the design-basis fire.

#### 4.2.4.3 Methods of Analysis

##### 4.2.4.3.1 General Methods of Analysis

The methods of analysis in this section are permitted for determining structural capacities at fire conditions. The thermal response of the structure shall be developed and refined in accordance with the provisions of Section 4.2.1 through Section 4.2.3 for the fire, heat transfer, thermal properties, and material properties at elevated temperatures.

Elevated thermal response shall in the design-basis fire.

The thermal response results in forces and deformations in the structural system that may be developed using the methods described in accordance with Section 4.2.4.3.2, as well as moment magnification effects provided in the *Specification*. Material properties at elevated temperatures shall be incorporated temperature-dependent thermal properties and structural material properties in accordance with Section 4.2.3. Magnification effects shall be determined in accordance with Section 4.2.4. Boundary conditions shall be determined on the effects of thermal expansion, and force deformations. Boundary conditions and joint deformations shall be adequate to ensure the requirements of the proposed structural design. Material properties shall be as given in Section 4.2.3.

The resulting analysis shall consider all relevant limit states, such as excessive deflections, connection failures, and overall and local buckling.

##### 4.2.4.3.2 Simple Methods of Analysis

The methods of analysis in this section apply to evaluating the performance of individual members at elevated temperatures during exposure to fire.

Support and restraint conditions (forces, moments, and boundary conditions) at normal temperature may be assumed to remain unchanged throughout the fire exposure.

a) Tension members

It is permitted to model the thermal response of a tension member using a one-dimensional heat transfer equation with heat input from the design-basis fire defined in Section 4.2.1.

The design strength of a tension member shall be determined using the provisions of Chapter D with aluminum properties as given in Section 4.2.3 and assuming a uniform temperature over the cross section using the temperature equal to the maximum aluminum temperature.

b) Compression members

It is permitted to model the thermal response of a compression member using a one-dimensional heat transfer equation with heat input from the design-basis fire defined in Section 4.2.1.

The design strength of a compression member shall be determined using the provisions of Chapter E with aluminum properties as given in Section 4.2.3.

c) Flexural members

It is permitted to model the thermal response of a flexural member using a one-dimensional heat transfer equation to calculate bottom flange temperature and to assume that this bottom flange temperature is constant over the depth of the member. Heat input shall be determined from the design-basis fire defined in Section 4.2.1.

The design strength of a flexural member shall be determined using the provisions of Chapter F with aluminum properties as given in Section 4.2.3.

#### 4.2.4.4 Design Strength

The design strength shall be determined in accordance with Section B.3. The nominal strength $R_n$ shall be determined using the material properties given in Section 4.2.3 at the temperature developed during the design-basis fire.

## 4.3 DESIGN BY QUALIFICATION TESTING

### 4.3.1 Qualification Standards

Structural members and components in aluminum structures shall be qualified for the rating period in conformance with ASTM E119.

### 4.3.2 Restrained Construction

A restrained condition exists when the surrounding or supporting structure is capable of resisting actions caused by thermal expansion throughout the range of anticipated elevated temperatures.

# Appendix 5 Evaluation of Existing Structures

## 5.1 GENERAL PROVISIONS

These provisions apply to evaluating the strength or serviceability condition of existing aluminum structures in structures meeting or exceeding acceptable limits.

Load-carrying components shall be investigated to the extent that strength can be verified as meeting the criteria specified, but not restricted to: (a) determination of the composition of a specific or as built condition (b) determination of the deformation of structural members and connections, (c) analysis of both structural analysis and load testing.

## 5.2 MATERIAL TESTS

### 5.2.1 Wrought and Cast Products

Unless aluminum alloys and tempers used in the structure are identified from records, specimens shall be cut from the structure and tests conducted to determine the alloy and temper.

Mechanical properties of alloys and tempers shall be determined in accordance with the provisions of Appendix 1. Tests to determine ultimate strength, yield strength, and elongation to determine the temper.

### 5.2.2 Welds

If the alloy performance depends on existing welds:

a) The alloy metals used in the structure shall be determined from records or chemical analysis or representative samples of weld metal.

b) If welds do not require the visual inspection criteria of Section J.8 or do not meet criteria specified in Section J.8, the minimum properties shall be used and tested in accordance with ASTM E10-99(2004).

### 5.2.3 Bolts and Rivets

Unless bolt or rivet strength can be determined from records or visual inspection, representative samples shall be tested in accordance with ASTM F606/F606M.

## 5.3 EVALUATION BY STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS

### 5.3.1 Dimensions

Dimensions and the condition of structural members and connections shall be determined from records and/or from a field survey.

### 5.3.2 Strength Evaluation

Loads shall be determined in accordance with Section B.2. If the strength analysis methods to be adopted shall be determined to comply with the provisions of this *Specification*, account of members and connections shall be taken for the development of required strength per the provisions of this *Specification*.

### 5.3.3 Serviceability Evaluation

Deflections and/or vibrations under service loads shall be evaluated in accordance with Section L.

## 5.4 EVALUATION BY LOAD TESTING

If resistance cannot be calculated using a written testing plan proposed when the evaluation is to be based on load tests:

a) The loads to be applied incrementally in accordance with the testing plan. The load strength of the structure or member to be based on applying a load of 1.5L to 1.5D + 1.5L, where $D$ is the dead load and $L$ is the design live rating $L$ shall represent $L_n$ or $R$.

b) No Load

The normal load to be carried are none, inclusive of $R$ = dead load plus live load = normal load for construction.

More severe load combinations shall be used where applicable to the design of the structure and its components.

The structure shall be visually inspected for signs of distress at the test load with a load of 1.0D plus live load for a period of 24 hours after the removal of the load. The test load shall not exceed a value that poses unacceptable risk to testing personnel. More severe combinations may be used where warranted to investigate for the structure being tested.

c) Acceptance Criteria

The evaluation shall be documented by a written report using Section 5.3 or permitted by a combination of structural analysis, field data, and test testing.

Where testing was performed, the report shall include a combination of structural analysis and load testing, as well as dimensions of critical sections where testing was undertaken. Where testing was performed, maximum deflections shall be conducted in accordance with Appendix 1 if load testing relationships observed.

Test dimensional information provide basic material test data and properties extracted from the structure cannot be established by available strength of the member, connections, or structure.

When the available strength is determined by test of the date for its evaluation was performed.

# Appendix 6 Member Stability Bracing

This appendix provides bracing strength and stiffness specifications for bracing at each point in a column, beam, or beam-column.

## 6.1 GENERAL PROVISIONS

Bracing systems shall have the strength and stiffness specified in this section. Bracing shall be designed such that the sum of the required strengths of all members being braced, based on the effective bracing forces provided by the bracing, shall be equal to or greater than the required strengths of all members needed to meet the requirements in Sections 6.2 and 6.3. Beams shall be designed such that the sum of forces associated to meet the requirements in Sections 6.2 and 6.3 does not exceed the available strength. The required brace strength shall be determined based on the provisions of Section 6.2.1.

a) The required brace strength and stiffness can be obtained from an analysis of the structural system or from Section 6.2.3 and 6.3, and the distance between the braced points.

Where all members at a braced point have the same $L_b / r_y$ ratio (≤ 2λₒ), and/or:

a) For either of the two ubiquitous strength members will not require an analysis. Members are exempted from these beam analysis. The required brace strength is determined from the provisions of Section 6.2.3 and the distance between braced points.

$$k_v = \frac{1}{\phi}\left(\frac{2P_r}{L_b}\right)$$ (6-2 LRFD)

where

- $k_v = 0.075 (LRFD)$
- $P_r$ = required strength of the $i$th column in the panel under consideration

### 6.2.2 Point Bracing

The required strength of the bracing system shall have the strength and stiffness specified in this section. The connection of the bracing system to the member shall have the strength specified in Section 6.3.1.2 for a point brace at that location.

The required shear strength of the bracing system is

$$V_{bs} = 0.01M_r C_b / h_o$$ (6-5)

The required stiffness of the bracing system is

$$\beta_{bs} = \frac{1}{\phi}\left(\frac{4M_r C_b}{L_b h_o}\right)$$ (6-6 LRFD)

$$\beta_{bs} = \Omega \left(\frac{4M_r C_b}{L_b h_o}\right)$$ (6-6 ASD)

where

- $\phi = 0.75 (LRFD)$
- $\Omega = 2.00 (ASD)$
- $h_o$ = distance between flange centroids
- $C_b$ = 1.0 except $C_b$ = 2.0 for the brace closest to the in-flection point in a beam subject to double curvature
- $L_b$ = unbraced length within the panel under consideration
- $M_r$ = required flexural strength of the beam in the panel under consideration

### 6.2.3 Torsional Bracing

The required strength of end and intermediate point braces in the direction perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of the column is:

$$P_{bs} = 0.02M_r C_b / h_o$$ (6-7)

The required stiffness of the brace is

$$\beta_{bs} = \frac{1}{\phi}\left(\frac{10 M_r C_b}{L_b h_o}\right)$$ (6-8 LRFD)

$$\beta_{bs} = \Omega \left(\frac{10M_r C_b}{L_b h_o}\right)$$ (6-8 ASD)

where

- $\phi = 0.75 (LRFD)$
- $\Omega = 2.00 (ASD)$
- $h_o$ = distance between flange centroids
- $C_b$ = 1.0 except $C_b$ = 2.0 for the brace closest to the inflection point in a beam subject to double curvature
- $L_b$ = unbraced length adjacent to the point brace
- $M_r$ = largest of the required flexural strengths of the beam within the unbraced lengths adjacent to the point brace

When the unbraced lengths adjacent to a point brace have different $M_r/L_b$ values, the larger value shall be used to determine the required brace stiffness.

For intermediate point bracing of an individual beam, $L_b$ in Equation 6-8 need not be taken less than the maximum effective length, $L_e$, permitted for the beam based on the required flexural strength, $M_r$.

### 6.3.2 Torsional Bracing

Torsional bracing shall be attached at any cross-section location, and need not be attached near the compression flange.

#### 6.3.2.1 Point Bracing

The required flexural strength of the brace about the longitudinal axis of the beam is

$$M_{br} = 0.02M_{re}$$ (6-9)

The required flexural stiffness of the brace is

$$\beta_{br} = \frac{\beta_f}{1 - \frac{\beta_f}{\beta_{sec}}}$$ (6-10)

where

$$\beta_f = \frac{1}{\phi}\left(\frac{2.4 L M_r^2}{nEI_{eff} C_b^2}\right)$$ (6-11 LRFD)

$$\beta_f = \Omega \left(\frac{2.4 L M_r^2}{nEI_{eff} C_b^2}\right)$$ (6-11 ASD)

$$\beta_{sec} = \frac{3.3E}{h_o}\left(\frac{1.5h_o t_c^3}{12} + \frac{L_b h_t^3}{12}\right)$$ (6-12)

where

- $\phi = 0.75 (LRFD)$
- $\Omega = 3.00 (ASD)$
- $I_{eff}$ = effective out-of-plane moment of inertia = $I_c + (t/c)I_t$
- $I_c$ = moment of inertia of the compression flange about the y-axis
- $I_t$ = moment of inertia of the tension flange about the y-axis
- $L$ = span length
- $n$ = number of braced points within the span
- $M_r/C_b$ = maximum value of the required flexural strength of the beam divided by the moment gradient factor, within the unbraced lengths adjacent to the point brace
- $t_c$ = beam web thickness
- $t_b$ = beam web stiffener thickness
- $c$ = distance from the neutral axis to the extreme compressive fibers
- $t$ = distance from the neutral axis to the extreme tensile fibers
- $h_t$ = stiffener width for one-sided stiffeners (use twice the individual width for pairs of stiffeners)
- $\beta_T$ = overall brace system required stiffness
- $\beta_{sec}$ = web distortional stiffness, including the effect of web transverse stiffeners, if any
- $M_r$ = largest of the required flexural strengths of the beam within the unbraced lengths adjacent to the point brace

Web stiffeners shall extend the full depth of the braced member and shall be attached to the flange if the torsional brace is also attached to the flange. Web stiffeners are permitted to stop short of the beam flange by a distance equal to $4t_c$ if the torsional brace is not attached to the flange.

#### 6.3.2.2 Continuous Bracing

For continuous torsional bracing:

a) The brace strength per unit length along the beam shall be 0.02$M_r$ divided by the maximum unbraced length permitted for the beam based on the required flexural strength, $M_r$. The required flexural strength, $M_r$, is the maximum value throughout the beam span.

b) The brace stiffness per unit length shall be given by Equations 6-10 and 6-11 with $L/n = 1.0$.

c) The web distortional stiffness shall be taken as:

$$\beta_{sec} = \frac{3.3EI_w^3}{12h_o}$$ (6-13)

## 6.4 BEAM-COLUMN BRACING

For bracing of beam-columns, the required strength and stiffness for the axial force shall be determined as specified in Section 6.2, and the required strength and stiffness for the flexure shall be determined as specified in Section 6.3. The values so determined shall be combined as follows:

a) When panel bracing is used, the required strength is the sum of the values determined using Equations 6-1 and 6-5, and the required stiffness is the sum of the values determined using Equations 6-2 and 6-6.

b) When point bracing is used, the required strength is the sum of the values determined using Equations 6-3 and 6-7, and the required stiffness is the sum of the values determined using Equations 6-4 and 6-8. In Equations 6-4 and 6-8, $L_b$ for beam-columns shall be taken as the actual unbraced length; the provisions in Sections 6.2.2 and 6.3.1.2 that $L_b$ need not be taken less than the maximum permitted effective length based on $P_r$ and $M_r$ shall not be applied.

c) When torsional bracing is provided for flexure in combination with point bracing for the axial force, the required strength and stiffness shall be combined or distributed in a manner that is consistent with the resistance provided by the equivalent(s) of the actual bracing details.

d) When the combined stress effect from axial force and flexure results in net compression to both flanges, either lateral bracing shall be added to both flanges or both flanges shall be laterally restrained by a combination of lateral and torsional bracing.

Torsional bracing shall be attached at any cross-section location, and need not be attached near the compression flange.

The required flexural strength of the brace about the longitudinal axis of the beam is

$$M_{br} = 0.02M_{ro}$$ (6-9)

$$\beta_{br} = \frac{\beta_f}{\left(1 - \frac{\beta_f}{\beta_{sec}}\right)}$$ (6-10)

where

$$\beta_f = \frac{1}{\phi}\left(\frac{2.4LM_r^2}{nEI_{eff}C_b^2}\right)$$ (6-11 LRFD)

$$\beta_f = \Omega\left(\frac{2.4LM_r^2}{nEI_{eff}C_b^2}\right)$$ (6-11 ASD)

$$\beta_{sec} = \frac{3.3E}{h_o}\left(\frac{1.5h_o t_w^3}{12} + \frac{t_w b^3}{12}\right)$$ (6-12)

where
- $\phi$ = 0.75 (LRFD)
- $\Omega$ = 3.00 (ASD)
- $I_{eff}$ = effective out-of-plane moment of inertia = $I_w + (t/c)I_f$
- $I_w$ = moment of inertia of the compression flange about the y-axis
- $I_f$ = moment of inertia of the tension flange about the y-axis
- $L$ = span length
- $n$ = number of braced points within the span
- $M_r/C_b$ = maximum value of the required flexural strength of the beam divided by the moment gradient factor, within the unbraced lengths adjacent to the point brace
- $t_w$ = beam web thickness
- $t_c$ = beam web stiffener thickness
- $c$ = distance from the neutral axis to the extreme compressive fibers
- $t$ = distance from the neutral axis to the extreme tensile fibers
- $b_f$ = stiffener width for one-sided stiffeners (use twice the individual width for pairs of stiffeners)
- $\beta_f$ = overall brace system required stiffness
- $\beta_{sec}$ = web distortional stiffness, including the effect of web transverse stiffeners, if any
- $M_r$ = largest of the required flexural strengths of the beam within the unbraced lengths adjacent to the point brace

Web stiffeners shall extend the full depth of the braced member and shall be attached to the flange if the torsional brace is also attached to the flange. Web stiffeners are permitted to stop short of the beam flange by a distance equal to $4t_w$ if the torsional brace is not attached to the flange.
