# 1. SCOPE

These guidelines apply to the use of aluminum in roofing, flashing and other sheet metal work in building construction. They do not apply to proprietary or pre-formed sheet metal systems or products such as horizontal siding, fascia and soffit systems, curtain wall systems, or corrugated or ribbed roofing and siding.

### 2.1.2 Thickness

Sheet thickness shall be sufficient for the intended use, but not less than the minimum nominal thickness listed in Table 1. The thickness of shingles and proprietary roofing systems shall be determined for specific applications on the basis of load tests.

### 2.1.3 Dimensional Tolerances

Tolerances on sheet dimensions shall conform to *Aluminum Standards and Data*.

### 2.1.4 Finish

Sheet shall be mill finish unless otherwise specified. Embossed, anodized, or painted sheet shall be acceptable provided it has the necessary strength and formability for the intended purpose.

## 2.2 BUILDING PAPER AND ROOFING FELT

Building paper or roofing felt used where condensation occurs or over poorly vented spaces shall be vapor barriers conforming to Federal Specifications UU-B-790A, Type I, Grade A; HH-R-590A, Type II, Class C; or polyethylene not less than 4 mils (0.1 mm) thick conforming to Federal Specifications L-P-378B or L-P-512A.

Building paper or roofing felt over well ventilated spaces need not be water proof but shall be water repellant and shall conform to Federal Specifications UU-B-790A, Type I, Grade C or D; HH-R-590A, Type II; or HH-R-595B, Type I or II.

Building paper and roofing felt that contains additives of heavy metals or chemicals corrosive to aluminum shall not be used.

Building paper or roofing felt shall have a minimum weight of 15 lb (6.8 kg) and shall be overlapped at least 2 in. (50 mm) so as to shed water and shall be secured along the laps with large flat headed aluminum nails spaced no farther than 6 in. (150 mm) on centers.

## 2.3 SEALANTS

### 2.3.1 One Part

One part synthetic or rubber base sealants shall conform to Federal Specification TT-S-230A(1). Use shall conform to manufacturers' specifications.

### 2.3.2 Two Part

Two part synthetic or rubber base sealants shall conform to Federal Specification TT-S-00227E(1) or ANSI A116.1. Use shall conform to manufacturers' specifications.

## 2.4 ELASTIC CEMENT

Elastic cement shall conform to Federal Specification SS-C-153.

## 2.5 FASTENERS

### 2.5.1 Cleats

Cleats shall be aluminum of the same alloy, temper, and nominal thickness as the sheet unless otherwise specified. Cleats shall be at least 2 in. (50 mm) wide and long enough to be fully incorporated into the seam with the cleat end folded back over the nail heads. They shall be fastened securely with two aluminum nails placed parallel to the seam.

### 2.5.2 Nails

Nails shall be 5056 or 6061 aluminum and conform to ASTM F 1667.

### 2.5.3 Screws, Bolts, and Nuts

Screws and bolts shall be 6061-T6 or 2024-T4 aluminum except that 2024-T4 fasteners shall not be used in

marine or corrosive industrial environments. Nuts shall be 6061-T6 or 6262-T9 aluminum. Alternately, screws, bolts and nuts shall be 300 series stainless steel. Washers shall be used under bolt and screw heads.

### 2.5.4 Washers

Washers shall be 1100-H18 aluminum or of the same material as the sheet or fasteners used. The minimum nominal thickness of washers shall be 0.040 in.(1 mm). Where water tightness is required an elastomeric washer shall be used with the metal washer.

### 2.5.5 Miscellaneous Hardware

Expansion inserts, plugs, anchors, and shields shall be wood, fiber, plastic, lead, or aluminum and shall be chosen to suit the environment of each application.

### 2.5.6 Rivets

Rivets shall be 1100-H14 aluminum unless otherwise specified. Rivets shall have a shank diameter of at least 0.187 in. (4.75 mm) and sufficiently long to form a proper head. Blind rivets shall be used only where maximum water tightness, strength, or corrosion resistance is not required. Blind rivets shall be 1100 sleeve, 5056 mandrel; 5050 sleeve, 5056 mandrel; 5052 sleeve, 5056 mandrel; 5052 sleeve, 7178 mandrel, 5056 sleeve, 1020 steel mandrel; stainless steel; or monel.

## 2.6 PAINT

Paint for back painting aluminum shall be bituminous paint of the cut-back type conforming to specification MIL-C-450 B (1) or TT-C-494 or methacrylate type lacquers conforming to MIL-L-19537C (2).

# 3. SURFACE PREPARATION

## 3.1 GENERAL

All surfaces upon which aluminum sheet is to be placed shall be smooth, even and free of projections and hollows. The surface shall be dry before and during the placing of the aluminum. For wood surfaces the lumber shall be of good quality, well seasoned, straight, and free of knotholes and splits. It shall be laid with joints true, even, and firmly attached and all fastener heads flush with the top surface. On masonry surfaces adequate provision shall be made for receiving fasteners in accordance with the plans for sheet metal work.

## 3.2 DISSIMILAR METALS

Galvanic corrosion of aluminum caused by contact with dissimilar metals shall be prevented by proper design considerations and installation procedures. Water that has come in contact with copper, brass, or bronze shall be directed away from aluminum.

## 3.3 WOOD

Wood sheathing or wood surfaces to be covered with aluminum shall be covered with building paper or roofing felt (Section 2.2) or the wood shall be painted with two coats of good quality exterior type paint or the aluminum shall be back painted (Section 2.6).

### 3.4 CONCRETE AND MASONRY

Concrete or masonry surfaces in contact with aluminum (Section 2.1) or the aluminum shall be back painted (Section 2.6).

# 4. JOINING

## 4.1 MECHANICAL JOINTS

Mechanical seams and joints for aluminum shall be the same as for other metals. See Figure 1 for examples. Rivets and screws relative movement unless such seams are designed to handle deflection.

## 4.2 SOLDERED JOINTS

Aluminum shall not be soldered.

## 4.3 BRAZING

Brazing shall be done in the shop and flux residue shall be completely removed.

## 4.4 WELDING

Welding shall comply with AWS D1.2.

# 5. STANDING SEAM ROOFING

## 5.1 ROOF SLOPES

5.1.1 These specifications do not apply to roofs with slopes less than 3 in 12 (7:1).

## 5.2 UNDERLAYMENT

5.2.1 Underlayment should be applied according to local practice of roofing that exceed 10 ft (3.0 m). Nominal sheet thickness shall be 0.024 in. (0.6 mm) for residences and 0.032 in. (0.8 mm) for other than residential buildings. Sheets shall be secured by cleats spaced not more than the lesser of 12 in. (300 mm) and one-half the width of the sheet shall be secured at least 3 in. (100 mm) at each end. Alternate methods for attaching sheets to form a standing seam system should allow for thermal expansion.

### 5.3 STANDING SEAM

The latter end shall be formed into a standing seam. The upper end of each sheet shall be folded under 2 in. (50 mm). The lower end shall be formed into a standing seam providing drainage for condensation. Note: For flat roofs, or roofs with slopes less than 3 in. 12 (7:1), the lower end shall be folded and dressed flat to the standing seam. The hem or flat fold on the upper end of the sheet should be secured to support and the lower end shall be free to move.

## 5.5 CLEATS

Cleats shall be at least 2 in. (50 mm) wide and shall be spaced not more than 12 in. (300 mm) apart between centers. If the roof deck is a material other than wood, nailing shall be to provide to secure the cleats.

## 5.6 RIDGES AND HIPS

(See Figure 2). Ridges and hips shall be provided with standing seams constructed as for the main roof. Where standing seams of the main roof terminate at ridges or hips they shall be laid flat and folded into ridge or hip standing seams. Standing seams on opposite sides of ridges or hips shall be staggered to avoid excessive thicknesses of metal in the ridge or hip standing seam.

## 5.7 VALLEYS

Valleys shall be formed from aluminum sheets not exceeding 10 ft (3 m) in length of the same nominal thickness and alloy as used for the roof sheets. Each sheet shall lap the lower one at least 6 in. (150 mm) in the direction of drainage. The valley sheet shall extend at least 6 in. (150 mm) under the roof sheets on both sides. At the valley line adjacent to the lower edge of the roof sheets, a 0.75 in. (20 mm) double fold shall be made to engage a 0.75 in. (20 mm) single fold at the lower ends of the roof sheets. The

outer edge of the valley sheets shall be folded 0.5 in. (12 mm) outward and in these folds cleats shall be spaced not more than 24 in. (600 mm) apart between centers. Valley sheets shall be nailed along their top edge only.

Edge strips shall be continuous and shall be formed from sheets no longer than 10 ft (3 m); ends of adjacent lengths shall lap at least 1 in. (25 mm). The edge strip shall extend up the roof deck at least 4 in. (100 mm) and be secured with nails spaced not more than 4 in. (100 mm) apart along the upper edge. The lower edge shall be turned out 0.75 in. (20 mm) to form a drip edge. The edge strip shall not be face nailed.

## 6. BATTEN SEAM ROOFING

### 6.1 ROOF SLOPES

These specifications do not apply to roofs with slopes less than 4 in 12 (7:1).

### 6.2 BATTENS

(See Figure 3). Battens shall be aluminum or wood with minimum dimensions 1 in. wide and at least 1 in. (38 mm) high. Battens shall be attached to concrete roof decks by through-bolting and embedded expansion anchors, bolts or lead expansion inserts

### 6.3 UNDERLAYMENT

Roof sheets shall be no longer than 10 ft (3.0 m) and be installed according to normal roofing practices. Nominal sheet thickness shall be 0.024 in. (0.6 mm) for residences and 0.032 in. (0.8 mm) for other than residential applications. Sheets shall be space 3 in. (100 mm) apart on centers. The battens span 8 ft (100 mm) between battens is from 20 in. (500 mm) to 27 in. (700 mm). The width of sheets shall not exceed 24 in. (600 mm). Sides of sheets shall be turned up to heights of the batten and shall project over battens.

### 6.4 TRANSVERSE SEAMS

**6.4.1** (See Figures 1 and 3). For roofs with slopes 4 in. 12 (7:1) or greater, the transverse seam at each sheet shall be folded under 2 in. (50 mm). The fold shall be at the lower end of each sheet shall be dressed flat to form a hem and then shall be turned up to heights of the upper end of each sheet in Section 6.4.1 and 6.4.2, roof sheets shall be turned under and dressed flat with the batten.

For roofs with slopes less than 4 in. 12 (7:1), both lower and upper ends of each sheet in Section 6.4.1 and 6.4.2 sheets shall be secured shall be formed over it. The fold shall be a minimum distance of one half the sheet length in adjacent roof sheets.

**6.4.2** (See Figures 1 and 3). For roofs with slopes 3 in. 12 (7:1) but less than 4 in. 12 (7:1), the lower end of each sheet shall be dressed flat to form a 5/8 inch lap seam that shall be formed over it. The fold shall be secured at its outer edge by inserting into it a strip of doubled-over felt. The top folded edge by rivet spaced not more than 6 in. (150 mm) apart. The 0.75 in. (20 mm) fold on the lower end of the sheet shall be made as specified in Section 6.4.1 and the upper end of each sheet shall be folded over 3 in. (75 mm). Alternatively, transverse seams shall be made as specified in Section 6.4.1 and the upper end of the underlying sheet. Alternatively, transverse seams shall be made as specified in Section 6.4.1 and staggered a distance of one half the sheet length in adjacent roof sheets.

### 6.5 CLEATS

Cleats shall be not less than 2 in. (50 mm) wide and shall be spaced not more than 12 in. (300 mm) apart on centers. U-shaped cleat passing under the batten shall be used.

### 6.6 COVER STRIPS

Cover strips formed of the same alloy and nominal thickness as used for the roof sheets shall be 4 in. (100 mm) wide. Cover strips shall lock into the roof sheets and extend over the battens. Cover strips shall secure in place with the gable end. Where battens occur at gable ends, a cover strip shall lock into the roof sheets and extend over the battens and down the face of the gable end to hook 0.75 in. (20 mm) over a previously placed continuous edge strip that is secured to the deck with aluminum nails spaced no more than 4 in. (100 mm) apart.

Aluminum roof shingles shall be of the size and shape shown on the drawings. Installation shall be in accordance with manufacturer's specifications.

### 6.7 HIPS AND RIDGES

(See Figure 3). Hip and ridge cover sections shall be aluminum covered battens similar to the roof battens. At their intersections with the roof battens of ridges or hips shall be installed over hips and ridges to finish with cap. Alternate methods of finishing hips and ridges similar to those shown in Section 5.6.1.

### 6.8 VALLEYS

Valleys shall be formed from aluminum sheets not exceeding 10 ft (3 m) of the same gauge and alloy as used for the roof sheets. Each valley sheet shall lap the lower one at least 6 in. (150 mm) in the direction of drainage. Each valley sheet should extend at least 6 in. (150 mm) under the roof sheets. At the valley line adjacent to the lower edge of the roof sheets should extend at least 6 in. (150 mm) made to engage a 0.75 in. (12 mm) single fold at the lower end of the roof sheets. The outer edge of the valleys sheet shall be folded 0.5 in. (12 mm) outward and these folds cleats shall be spaced not more than 24 in. (600 mm) on centers. The under edge of wood battens shall be notched to permit the folded edge of valleys shall be nailed along the edge only.

# 9. VALLEYS

## 9.1 OPEN VALLEYS

(See Figure 5). Valley flashing shall be formed of aluminum sheets not exceeding 10 ft (3 m) in length. Each valley sheet shall lap the lower one at least 6 in. (150 mm) in the direction of drainage. The valley sheet shall extend at least 6.8 in. (12 mm) into the closing. Uphill sheets shall overlap downhill sheets by at least 2 in. (50 mm). The upper end of each valley sheet at the ridge and the lower end shall be secured with aluminum cleats spaced no farther than 12 in. (300 mm) apart. The outer edges of the valley sheet shall be curved upward. The outer portion of the shingle at the valley line shall form a 1.5 in. (38 mm) lip over the edge of the valley and shall fit 1 in (25 mm) minimum into the valley before the ridge and the lap edge of the valley shall be increased to 8 in. (200 mm) for horizontal runs.

## 9.2 CLOSED VALLEYS

(See Figure 5). Separate pieces of aluminum sheet flashing shall be installed under adjacent shingles material. The number of pieces shall be kept to a minimum. Valley flashing shall extend 7 in. (180 mm) in each direction from the center where the roof slope is more than 4 in 12 (17.5) and 18 in. (450 mm) where the roof slope is less than or equal to 4 in 12 (17.5). The outer edges of valley flashing shall be 0.5 in. (12 mm) short of the butt line of the shingle in the succeeding course. Each piece of flashing shall be embedded in plastic cement on the upper and lower edge.
