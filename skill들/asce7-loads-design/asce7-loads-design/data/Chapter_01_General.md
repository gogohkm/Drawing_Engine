# CHAPTER 1
# GENERAL

## 1.1 SCOPE

This standard provides minimum loads, hazard levels, associated load combinations and load factors for use in the design of buildings, structures, and their nonstructural components that are subject to building code requirements. Load combinations, factors, and modifications in this document are based on accepted load criteria that have been used successfully for providing acceptable performance. This standard continues to use allowable strength design (ASD) load combinations in an expanded format to account for environmental loads and their combinations. Load combinations in this format, together with relevant strength reduction factors (phi factors) specified by the applicable material specifications are provided in an expanded format to account for environmental hazards. These combinations and factors have been tested in practice over many decades.

**ADDITION:** An extension or increase in floor area, number of stories, or height of a building or structure.

**AGRICULTURAL BUILDING:** A structure designed and constructed to house agricultural equipment, tools, feed or harvested crops, and livestock that is used primarily for agricultural purposes. The building or structure may be subject to special consideration because of unique or unusual occupancy characteristics.

**ALTERATION:** Any change, modification, or addition to the structural system of an existing building or structure other than repair or a change of occupancy that affects the safety of the building or structure.

**ALLOWABLE STRESS DESIGN:** A method of proportioning structural members for permitted loads at stresses not exceeding those specified in this standard.

**AUTHORITY HAVING JURISDICTION (AHJ):** The organization, political subdivision, office, or individual charged with the responsibility to provide permits and authorization for the construction, alteration, or occupancy of buildings and for enforcement of building codes in accordance with the jurisdiction established by the organization.

**BALLAST:** Permanent materials placed on or attached to a photovoltaic system to provide additional protection against the uplift forces. For elevated roof structures such as BIPV/BAPV, ballast systems are placed at the edge to provide ballasting at roof edges and interior locations of photovoltaic systems such that the system maintains stability. The material is not covered by roofing materials and provides no structural load path through the primary structural system.

**DESIGNATED NONSTRUCTURAL SYSTEM:** A nonstructural system not located in an essential facility and for which the performance-based procedures of Chapter 1 are applied that is used primarily to support or house architectural, mechanical, or electrical building services and equipment, and that serves (1) equipment, and is (2) explicitly or implicitly providing vertical or horizontal access.

**ESSENTIAL FACILITIES:** Buildings and other structures that are intended to remain operational in the event of extreme environmental loading from wind, snow, or earthquakes. Such facilities are categorized as Risk Category IV and described in Section 1.5.

**FACTORED LOAD:** The product of the nominal load and a load factor.

### 1.6.1 Load Test Procedure Specified Elsewhere

Where the applicable building code or a material standard approved by the Authority Having Jurisdiction includes an applicable load test procedure and acceptance criteria, the load test shall be conducted in accordance with that code or material standard.

### 1.6.2 Load Test Procedure Not Specified Elsewhere

In the absence of an applicable load test procedure within the building code or an applicable material standard approved by the Authority Having Jurisdiction, a load test procedure shall be developed and overseen by a registered design professional, who will also be responsible for interpreting the results of the load test. At a minimum, the applied load shall be determined using the load factors specified in the basic combinations in Section 2.3.1. The applied load shall simulate applicable gravity and deformation conditions relevant to the performance of the element or structure being tested. For statically loaded components, the test load shall be left in place for a period of at least 1 h. For components that carry dynamic loads such as impact loads, the load shall be left in place for a period consistent with the component's actual function. For materials that have strengths that are dependent on load duration, the test load shall be adjusted to account for the difference in load duration of the test compared to the expected duration of the design loads being considered. The component or structure shall be considered to have satisfied the test requirements where the following criteria are met:

1. During and after the test, the component or structure remains stable.
2. Within 24 h after removal of the test load, the component or structure has recovered not less than 75% of the maximum deflection;
3. Deflection that remains after the load test (if any) does not substantively affect serviceability as determined by the registered design professional.
