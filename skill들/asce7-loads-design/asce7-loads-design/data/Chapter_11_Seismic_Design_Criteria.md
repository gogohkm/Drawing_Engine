# Chapter 11: Seismic Design Criteria

**ASCE 7-22 Minimum Design Loads and Associated Criteria for Buildings and Other Structures**

---

*This chapter combines pages 161-172 from ASCE 7-22*

---

**11.1.1 Purpose.** Chapter 11 presents criteria for the design and construction of structures subject to earthquake ground motions. The specified earthquake loads are based upon postyield inelastic energy dissipation in the structure, and because of this, the provisions of this chapter are closely coordinated with the structure material design and construction standards for which load combinations that do not include earthquake forces are specified in Chapter 2.

**11.1.2 Scope.** Every structure and portion thereof, including nonstructural components, shall be designed and constructed to resist the effects of earthquake motions as prescribed by this section. Certain nonstructural components also require detailed consideration of seismic loading as described in Chapter 13; see also within the scope and with the design requirements of Sections 11.2, 11.3, and 15.3.1. The proposed site should also be investigated for liquefaction and soil strength loss, and slope stability issues from the seismic requirements of Chapter 11.

Existing structures shall undergo seismic evaluation and retrofit as prescribed by the authority having jurisdiction.

**11.1.3 Alternate Materials and Methods of Construction.** Alternate materials and methods of construction to those prescribed in the seismic requirements of this standard are permitted to be used subject to the approval of the authority having jurisdiction. Supporting data, when necessary, shall be submitted to and approved by the authority having jurisdiction.

Buildings whose purpose is to enclose equipment or machinery shall be permitted to be classified as nonbuilding structures if the structure is designed in accordance with Chapter 15.

**11.1.4 Alternate Materials and Methods of Construction Subject to the Limitations of the Authority Having Jurisdiction.** Procedures and methods of verification and substantiating evidence shall be submitted when requested to the authority having jurisdiction for approval. The quality assurance plan when specified, shall be subject to the approval of the authority having jurisdiction.

The use of alternative procedures and substantiating material provisions may be used for seismic design when prescribed by either Chapter 18 or Chapter 19 or other approved alternate procedures equivalent to the authority having jurisdiction based on reliable investigations that have been approved by the authority having jurisdiction.

The use of alternative methods shall be at least equivalent to the level of behavior expected based on the results of any proof test, investigation, or experience, and shall be reasonably safe for the intended occupancy.

**11.1.5 Quality Assurance.** Quality assurance for seismic force-resisting systems including system testing shall be in accordance with the materials reference standards for the system adopted by the authority having jurisdiction.

When the seismic design category is D, E, or F as addressed in Section 11.6, the quality assurance shall include the special inspection and test requirements of the Authority Having Jurisdiction for approval. The quality assurance plan when specified, shall be subject to the approval of the authority having jurisdiction.

### 11.2 DEFINITIONS

The following definitions apply only to the seismic provisions of this chapter.

**ACTIVE FAULT:** A fault determined to be active by the authority having jurisdiction from properly substantiated data (e.g., most recent mapping of active faults by the U.S. Geological Survey).

**ADDITION:** An increase in building area, aggregate floor area, height, or number of stories of a structure.

**ALTERATION:** A change in building area, aggregate floor area, height, or number of stories of a structure.

**APPENDAGE:** An architectural component that is attached to, supported by, or accessory to the structural system. Authority Having Jurisdiction: See Section 1.2.

Buildings whose purpose is to enclose equipment or machinery shall be permitted to be classified as nonbuilding structures if the structure is designed in accordance with Chapter 15.

**ATTACHMENTS:** Means by which nonstructural components, such as equipment, and their supports are mounted to structures. Connection to the seismic force-resisting system of the structure shall be distinguished from the fasteners, anchors, connections, and supports used to assemble the component and its mounting.

**BASE:** The level at which the horizontal seismic ground motions are assumed to be imparted to the structure.

**BASE SHEAR:** The total design lateral force or shear at the base.

**BASIC COMBINATIONS OF LOAD:** All load combinations shown in Section 2.3 and 2.4, except for load cases where the load combinations include both seismic and wind events, include slush and snow as a combined design load and concurrent loads, such as earthquakes and lateral drift or deformation provisions (such as story drift).

**BRACED FRAME:** A frame that connects columns and diagonals.

**BUCKLING-RESTRAINED BRACED FRAME:** A seismic force-resisting system comprised of a steel or concrete braced frame in which the members are configured to resist compression forces and tension forces. The compression members are restrained against buckling by being enclosed in a steel tube or reinforced concrete enclosure.

**BUILDING FRAME SYSTEM:** A seismic force-resisting system essentially complete space frame system providing support for the vertical loads to be shared by earthquake load resistance methods as defined in Chapter 12.

**CAST-IN-PLACE CONCRETE:** Concrete that is deposited in the location where it will harden as part of the structure, as distinguished from precast concrete.

**CHARACTERISTIC EARTHQUAKE:** An earthquake having a magnitude and other characteristics that are typical of earthquakes capable of being produced by a fault or source.

**COLLECTOR:** Diaphragm or shear wall that transfers lateral forces from a portion or portions of the structure to a diaphragm or shear wall or from a diaphragm to the vertical resisting elements.

**Component, Flexible:** A nonstructural component that has a fundamental period greater than 0.06 seconds.

**Component, Rigid:** A nonstructural component that has a fundamental period less than or equal to 0.06 seconds.

**COMPONENT, EQUIPMENT:** A mechanical or electrical component or element that is part of a mechanical or an electrical system within or without a building or nonbuilding structure.

**COMPONENT, NONSTRUCTURAL:** A part or element of an architectural, mechanical, or electrical system within or without a building or nonbuilding structure.

**Component, Permanently Attached:** A nonstructural component that is either affixed to the building or building structure using mechanical fasteners that can be removed and have removable horizontal support. The classification of a nonstructural component as a permanent attachment shall be based on a consideration of the long-term permanent nature of the connection or use.

**COMPOSITE SYSTEM:** Seismic force-resisting system composed of steel, reinforced concrete, or other precast or masonry members compositely connected, and have mounted horizontal properties.

**CONCRETE, PLAIN:** Concrete that does not comply with the requirements of reinforced concrete.

**CONCRETE, PRECAST:** A reinforced concrete component that has been cast in place and cured away from its final location and subsequently taken to its place of installation in its finished condition.

**CONNECTORS:** The portion of building construction required for the design of the seismic force-resisting system (or similar construction not intended for structural capacity) to transfer the horizontal forces induced by earthquakes located around the building system.

**DESIGN BASIS:** The basis of design information that is required to be used for the determination of strength and services for various structural components including locating and physical characteristics of the seismic requirements of this chapter.

**DEFORMABILITY:** The ratio of the ultimate deformation to limit deformation that is taken equal to 3.0 in the absence of substantiating test data in order to correct deficiencies without excessive limits.

**Design Basis Ground Motion:** A seismic shaking hazard specified for a site with a mean probability of exceedance of 1% in a 50-year period, as defined in Section 11.4. The design basis ground motion (DBE), are developed per the procedures in Section 21.2 and described as a design earthquake ground motion defined in Section 12.2.1.

**HIGH-DEFORMABILITY ELEMENT:** An element whose deformability is high enough such that collapse will not occur through the relatively high deformations.

**DESIGN EARTHQUAKE GROUND MOTION:** The design earthquake that has a probability of exceedance of 2/3 in 50 years, as defined in Section 11.4.2. For seismic hazard determination of vertical elements, the design basis ground motion shall be used to determine the design earthquake.

**DESIGN STORY DRIFT:** The difference in the horizontal displacements at the top and at the bottom of a story.

**Design Earthquake Displacement:** The story drift corresponding to the total horizontal displacement due to the seismic forces calculated per Section 12.8.6.1, is required to Section 12.8.6.

**DESIGNATED SEISMIC SYSTEMS:** Those nonstructural components that are listed in Chapter 13 and require qualified seismic force requirements or both.

**DIAPHRAGM:** Roof, floor, or other membrane or bracing system acting to transfer the lateral forces to the vertical resisting elements. See "Diaphragm, Flexible," "Diaphragm, Rigid."

**Diaphragm-Controlled Diaphragm:** Diaphragm with a flexible material diaphragm. (13.14), infers that materials with limited ductility or that impose severe lateral force demands on supporting elements may have reduced lateral resistance if one or more joints within the structural diaphragm.

**Diaphragm, Flexible:** Diaphragms that allow for unbalanced inelastic deformations or unbalanced forces in vertical seismic force-resisting elements located away from the edges or diaphragm perforations.

**Diaphragm, Rigid:** Diaphragm that does not distribute lateral loads among vertical elements in direct proportion to the stiffness of those elements.

**Diaphragm boundary element:** A diaphragm element at the edge of an opening within the diaphragm or at a boundary element at its perimeter (such as chords or collectors defined where the transfer or bracing or where drag reinforcement is needed to transfer forces into the vertical elements).

**DRIFT:** See "Design Story Drift" and "Design Earthquake Displacement."

**ELEMENT:** An architectural element or component that is part of a seismic force-resisting system or other subsystem or structure responsible to be applied load that is assumed to take axial perpendicular to the length of its span.

**EQUIPMENT, ELECTRICAL SUPPORT:** See "COMPONENT, EQUIPMENT."

**Design Earthquake Displacement:** The displacement of a story. Includes the story drift and interstory drift, as determined in Section 12.8.6 (see note at: 12.8.6.).

**FOUNDATION STRUCTURAL CAPACITY:** The design value used to distribute information that acts upon the foundation structure.

**FRAME:**

**Braced Frame:** An essentially vertical truss, or its equivalent, of the concentric or eccentric type that is provided in a building frame system or a braced frame system.

**Concentrically Braced Frame (CBF):** A braced frame in which the members are subjected primarily to axial forces. CBFs are categorized under Section 12.2.5 according to the desired ductile behavior of the braced frame (ordinary, intermediate, or special).

**Eccentrically Braced Frame (EBF):** A diagonally braced frame in which at least one end of each bracing member connects to a beam a short distance from a beam-to-column or beam-to-brace connection thereby producing bending in the beam between connections (the link).

**Moment Frame:** A frame in which members and joints resist forces primarily through flexure, categorized under Section 12.2.5 according to the desired ductile behavior of the moment frame (ordinary, intermediate, or special moment frames (OMFs)), and special moment frames (SMFs).

**LATERAL-FORCE-RESISTING SYSTEM:** That part of the structural system that has been considered in the design to provide the lateral resistance to the prescribed seismic forces.

**DIAPHRAGM:** A horizontal system with an essentially complete space frame providing support for the lateral loads, including seismic forces and transferring those vertical seismic forces to the vertical elements.

**FLEXIBLE DIAPHRAGM:** Diaphragm that permits the displacement of a critical section relative to other sections of the diaphragm above the supporting element.

**LINK:** Segments of a beam in an eccentrically braced frame that connect beam-to-column or beam-to-brace connections; the link is located between brace connections on separate beam segments.

**GLAZED STOREFRONT:** A nonload-bearing wall that is continuously supported laterally by the building structure.

**GRADE PLANE:** A plane defining the average level of finished ground surface surrounding at the elevation of the structure. In the plane as specified in this section shall be not less than 1.5 m (4.92 ft) measured horizontally from the structure, and not more from the structure or the property line nor where the finished ground level exists within 6 m (19.68 ft) of the structure.

**INSPECTION SPECIAL:** A systematic observation of the work by a qualified inspector to determine if the work is being performed in accordance with the applicable provisions.

**FRAME:**

**Braced Frame:** An essentially vertical truss, or its equivalent, of the concentric or eccentric type that is provided in a building frame system or a braced frame system to resist seismic forces.

**Eccentrically Braced Frame (EBF):** A diagonally braced frame in which at least one end of each bracing member connects to a beam a short distance from a beam-to-column or beam-to-beam connection, or from another bracing member-to-beam connection thereby producing bending in the beam between connections (the link).

**GLAZED STOREFRONT:** A nonloadbearing wall, not exceeding one story in height, with an in-plane aspect ratio not less than 6 to 1, that is laterally supported by the surrounding structure.

Where the finished ground level slopes away from the exterior walls, the reference plane shall be established by the lowest point within the area between the structure and the property line or, where the property line is less than 6 ft (1.83 m) from the structure, between the structure and a point 6 ft (1.83 m) from the structure.

**INSPECTION SPECIAL:** This observation of the work by an approved special inspector to determine that the work was being performed in accordance with approved construction documents and these standards in accordance with Section 1704.

**Special Inspections:** See Sections 1704.5 and 1705 for information regarding special inspections.

**INSPECTOR, SPECIAL:** A person approved by the An thority Having Jurisdiction, as defined in Section 1702.1, to perform special inspections in accordance with these standards. The special inspector may be retained to document field observations and provide test reports during construction.

**JOINT:** The geometric volume common to intersecting members.

**INTERMEDIATE MOMENT FRAME:** A frame system in which horizontal diaphragm transfers lateral loads to vertical seismic force-resisting elements. Each element shares the total vertical seismic force-resist loads in direct proportion to their stiffnesses, or by torsion primarily formed by a system of repetitive sections.

**LIGHT-FRAME CONSTRUCTION:** A method of construction in which floors, roofs, and walls are commonly framed by a system of repetitive wood or light-gage steel members or subfasteners.

**LIGHT-FRAME WALLS:** Walls using light-frame construction methods with wall coverings made of plywood, fiber board, gyp-sum wallboard, lath and plaster, gypsum, or other approved materials that provide the necessary shear resisting mechanism for design lateral loads.

**INTERMEDIATE REINFORCED MASONRY SHEAR WALL:** A masonry shear wall that contains reinforcing in both the horizontal and vertical directions.

**LINTEL:** The portion of a masonry wall above a door or window opening.

**LOAD AND RESISTANCE FACTOR DESIGN (LRFD):** A method of proportioning structural elements such that no applicable limit state is exceeded when the structure is subjected to the load combinations determined using appropriate load and resistance factors.

**SEISMIC DESIGN CATEGORY:** A classification assigned to a structure based on its Risk Category and the severity of the design earthquake ground motion at the site as defined in Section 11.6.

**SEISMIC FORCE-RESISTING SYSTEM:** That part of the structural system that has been considered in the design to provide the required resistance to the seismic forces as prescribed by this standard.

**SEISMIC FORCES:** The assumed forces prescribed herein, related to the response of the structure to earthquake ground motions, to be used in the design of the structure and its components.

**SEISMIC HOOK:** A 135-degree bend having a six-diameter extension that projects at least six diameters into the member, but not less than 3 in. (76 mm). Alternatively, a seismic hook may be a 90-degree bend that has a six-diameter extension that extends at least six diameters into the member, but not less than 3 in. (76 mm), and engages the longitudinal reinforcing (or anchor) where possible. Hooks less than 3 in. (76 mm), or when the 6d extension is blocked by another reinforcing bar, a stirrup shall be considered a seismic hook if the hook extends at least the greater of 6d or 3 in.

**SEISMICALLY ISOLATED STRUCTURE:** A structure that includes a seismic isolation system that substantially decouples the superstructure from the substructure and foundation. The seismic isolation system provides support and restoring force such that the fundamental periods are lengthened and energy dissipation is increased compared to a conventionally designed structure.

**SEISMIC USE GROUP:** A classification assigned to a structure based on its Risk Category, used to determine the required reliability of the seismic force-resisting system.

**SITE CLASS:** A classification assigned to a site based on the types of soils present and their engineering properties as defined in Section 20.3.

**SITE COEFFICIENTS Fa and Fv:** Site coefficients of the site that adjust ground motion maps to account for local site effects.

**SOFT STORY:** A story in which the lateral stiffness is less than 70 percent of that in the story above or less than 80 percent of the average stiffness of the three stories above.

**STACK EFFECT:** The difference in air pressure between the interior and exterior of a building caused by differences in temperature.

**STORAGE RACKS, STEEL:** A truss-work of assemblage, built-up from interconnected parts, supported on the floor or foundation with loads distributed horizontally and vertically to carry the effects between the vertical frames by floor diaphragms or other bracing. The braced vertical frames, commonly called uprights, constitute the vertical structural elements that serve as the primary vertical load carrying members and have limited or racks in one vertical plane, or side tiers, or racks in one vertical plane, or tiered racks.

**STORY:** That portion of a structure included between the upper surface of a floor and the upper surface of the floor or roof next above. It shall be measured as the vertical distance from the top of the floor to the top of the next floor or roof above.

**STORY ABOVE GRADE PLANE:** A story in which the floor or floor surface level is more than 6 ft (1.83 m) above grade plane or is greater than 12 ft (3.66 m) above the finished ground level at any point. A basement shall not be considered a story above grade plane unless the bottom of its floor is more than 6 ft (1.83 m) or more as measured from the grade plane to the diaphragm above.

**STORY, WEAK:** A weak story is one in which the story strength is less than 80 percent of that in the story above. The story strength is the total strength of all seismic force-resisting elements sharing the story shear in the considered direction.

**STORY DRIFT:** See "DISPLACEMENT AND DRIFT."

**STORY DRIFT RATIO:** The story drift divided by the story height.

**Design Story Drift (Δ):** The difference in horizontal displacements, as determined in Section 12.8.6, between the centers of mass at the top and bottom of the story under consideration.

**Design Story Drift Ratio:** The design story drift divided by the story height.

**STRUCTURAL HEIGHT (hn):** The vertical distance from the base to the highest level of the seismic force-resisting system of the structure.

**STRUCTURE:** An assemblage of interconnected components, such as beams, columns, slabs, pier walls, braced frames, walls or arches, and supports.

**SUBDIAPHRAGM:** A portion of a larger diaphragm used to transfer seismic loads to struts, anchorage of structural walls, or elements of the vertical seismic force-resisting system.

**TEMPORARY STRUCTURES:** Buildings, structures, or portions thereof which are intended to remain in place for a period of 180 days or less. For the purposes of calculation of seismic loads, occupancy loading for the duration of permitted use shall be assumed.

**TITLE DIAPHRAGM:** See Diaphragm (Section 11.2).

**UNREINFORCED MASONRY:** Masonry in which steel rods are not embedded in a manner necessary to resist tensile and shear loads, and which does not have prescribed reinforcement, steel percentages, and support ties.

**USGS SEISMIC DESIGN GEODATABASE:** A U.S. Geological Survey ground-motion interpolation tool that provides ground-motion parameters consistent with the seismic provisions developed in this standard for use in site-specific analyses. **VENEERS:** Facings or coatings of brick, concrete, stone, tile, or other materials secured at a backing.

### 11.8 GEOLOGIC HAZARDS AND GEOTECHNICAL INVESTIGATION

**11.8.1 Site Limitation for Seismic Design Categories E and F.** A structure assigned to Seismic Design Category E or F shall not be located where a known potential exists for an active fault to cause rupture of the ground surface at the structure.

**11.8.2 Geotechnical Investigation Report Requirements for Seismic Design Categories C through F.** A geotechnical investigation report for a structure assigned to Seismic Design Category C, D, E, or F shall include an evaluation of the following potential geologic and seismic hazards:

1. Slope instability;
2. Liquefaction;
3. Total and differential settlement; and
4. Surface displacement caused by seismic faulting or seismically induced lateral spreading or lateral flow.

The report shall contain recommendations for foundation design and for mitigation measures for the previously listed hazards.

**EXCEPTION:** Where approved by the Authority Having Jurisdiction, a site-specific geotechnical report is not required where these potential hazards are not present and sufficient data exists to address these hazards for the proposed construction.
