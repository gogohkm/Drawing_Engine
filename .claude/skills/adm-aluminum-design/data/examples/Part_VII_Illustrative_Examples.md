# Example 1
## ROD IN AXIAL TENSION
### Illustrating Section D.2

![Diagram showing an extruded round rod of Alloy 6061-T6 with tensile load of 4.5 k applied at both ends. The rod is shown as a long cylindrical member with arrows indicating tensile forces at each end.](Extruded round rod in tension with 4.5 k load)

**Figure 1**

## GIVEN:

1. Required ASD axial tension force: 4.50 k
2. Alloy-temper: 6061-T6
3. Product specification: ASTM B221

## FIND:

Diameter of smallest standard rod that will safely resist the load.

## SOLUTION:

From Part VI, Table 2-19, Section D.2: Allowable tensile stress: The net section allowable stress is less than the gross section allowable stress and the net section is less than the gross section, so only the net section needs to be considered.

$$F/\Omega = 19.5 \text{ ksi on the net section}$$

Given load $P = 4.50$ k

Required area $A = \frac{P}{F/\Omega} = (4.5 \text{ k})(19.5 \text{ k/in}^2) = 0.231 \text{ in}^2$

From Part VI, Table 5-3, the smallest diameter for $A = 0.231$ in² is 3/4 in. with a root area of 0.313 in², so use $D = 3/4$ in.

**NOTE:** Long slender members have little resistance to lateral loads. Therefore, tension members with large slenderness ratios $L/r$ should be avoided unless such members can also resist vibration and lateral loads such as wind, dead load, and the weight of workmen and equipment.

# Example 2
## RECTANGULAR BAR IN AXIAL TENSION
### Illustrating Section D.2

![Diagram showing a tension strap of Alloy 5052-H36, 1 inch wide, with tensile load of 1.20 k applied at both ends. The bar is shown as a flat rectangular member with arrows indicating tensile forces at each end.](Tension strap 1 inch wide with 1.20 k load)

**Figure 2**

## GIVEN:

1. Required ASD axial tension force: 1.20 k
2. Alloy-temper: 5052-H36
3. Product specification: ASTM B209

## FIND:

The thickness of a 1 in. wide bar which will safely resist the load. Assume that the ends of the bar are connected so that the net section is sufficiently large that it does not govern the tensile strength of the bar.

## SOLUTION:

From Section D.2, the allowable tensile strength for yielding in the gross section is

$$P_n = F_{ty} A_g / \Omega_t = 1.20 \text{ k}$$

Reading $F_{ty}$ from Table A.4.3 as 29 ksi, the required gross area is

$$A_g = P_n \Omega_t / F_{ty} = (1.20 \text{ k})(1.65)/(29 \text{ k/in}^2) = 0.0683 \text{ in}^2$$

The required thickness for 1.00 in. width is

$$t = 0.0683 \text{ in}^2/(1.00 \text{ in.}) = 0.0683 \text{ in.}$$

From Part V, Table 3, the minimum commonly used thickness $\geq 0.0683$ in. is $t = 0.071$ in.

# Example 3
## W-SHAPE IN FLEXURE AND SHEAR
### Illustrating Sections F.2, F.3, and G.2

![Diagram showing a W-Shape beam of Alloy 6061-T6 spanning 16 ft with continuous lateral support. The beam has four equal spans of 4'-0" each with vertical support spacing of 4 ft on center. A uniform load of 4.5 k/ft is applied across the entire length.](W-Shape continuous beam with uniform load over four spans)

**Figure 3**

## GIVEN:

1. Uniform load 4.50 k/ft including dead load
2. Beam length 16 ft with continuous lateral support
3. Vertical support spacing 4 ft o.c. (first support at end of beam)
4. Alloy-temper: 6061-T6
5. Product specification: ASTM B221

## FIND:

The size of the lightest Aluminum Association standard W-shape that will safely support the load using ASD.

## SOLUTION:

From Part VI Beam Formulas Case 43 continuous beam of four equal spans, uniformly distributed load:

Load $w = (4.50 \text{ k/ft})( \text{ft} /12 \text{ in.}) = 0.375 \text{ k/in.}$

The maximum required bending moment is

$$M = -168 wL^2/1568 = -168(0.375 \text{ k/in})(48 \text{ in})^2/1568$$
$$= -92.6 \text{ in-k}$$

The negative sign for the bending moment $M$ indicates that the top flange of the beam is in tension. The point of maximum stress is at the first interior support.

Section F.1 gives a safety factor of 1.95 for the rupture limit state and 1.65 for all other limit states.

The allowable moment for the limit state of yielding given in Section F.2 is $M_n/\Omega = ZF_{cy}/\Omega$, assuming that for a W-shape, $Z < 1.58$. Using $F_{cy} = 35$ ksi (see Tables A.4.1 and A.4.3), $\Omega = 1.65$, and setting the allowable yield moment equal to the required moment:

$$ZF_{cy}/\Omega = Z(35 \text{ k/in}^3)/1.65 = 92.6 \text{ in-k}$$

gives $Z = 4.36 \text{ in}^3$.

The allowable moment for the limit state of rupture given in Section F.2 is $M_n/\Omega = ZF_{tu}/k_t/\Omega$; using $F_{tu} = 38$ ksi and $k_t = 1.0$ (see Table A.4.3), $\Omega = 1.95$, and setting the allowable moment equal to the required moment:

$$ZF_{tu}/k_t/\Omega = Z(38 \text{ k/in}^3)/1.0/1.95 = 92.6 \text{ in-k}$$

gives $Z = 4.75 \text{ in}^3$. Since this exceeds the plastic modulus required for the yield limit state, use $Z = 4.75 \text{ in}^3$ to select a trial beam. The smallest Aluminum Association standard W-shape for which $Z > 4.75 \text{ in}^3$ is an AW 5 x 3.70, for which $Z = 6.31 \text{ in}^3$

Now check the beam for local buckling according to Section F.3.

a) The flange is in uniform compression, so the strength is given in Section B.5.4. Section B.5.4.1 addresses flat elements supported on one edge; from Part VI, Table 2-19

$$b/t = (3.50 - 0.19)/[(2)(0.32)] = 5.2 < 6.7 = \lambda_1, \text{ so}$$
$$F/\Omega = 21.2 \text{ ksi}$$

Since the flange slenderness is less than $\lambda_1$, deducting the web-flange fillets from the flange width will not change the flange's local buckling stress.

b) The web is in flexure so the strength is given by Section B.5.5.1, flat elements in flexure supported on both edges. Since the beam is symmetric about the bending axis, $c_1 = -c_2$, and $m = 0.65$.

$$b/t = (5.00 - 2(0.32))/0.19 = 22.9 < \lambda_1 = 33.1, \text{ so}$$

$$F/\Omega = 31.8 \text{ ksi}$$

For an AW 5 x 3.70, with a section modulus $S = 5.58 \text{ in}^3$, the

$$f = M/S = (92.6 \text{ in-k})/(5.58 \text{ in}^3) = 16.6 \text{ ksi} < 21.2 \text{ ksi}$$

so the AW 5 x 3.70 is acceptable for local buckling using the limiting element method (Section F.3.3).

For the limit states of shear yielding and buckling, web shear is addressed by Section G.2, flat webs supported on both edges.

$$b/t = 22.9 < 35.3 = \lambda_1, \text{ so } F_s/\Omega = 12.7 \text{ ksi}$$

For the limit state of shear rupture, Table 2-19 gives an allowable stress of 11.7 ksi.

From Part VI Beam Formulas Case 43, continuous beam of four equal spans.

$$V = 17wL/28 = 17(0.375 \text{ k/in.})(48 \text{ in.})/28 = 10.9 \text{ k},$$
maximum web shear

The required web area is

$$A = V/(F_s/\Omega) = (10.9 \text{ k})(11.7 \text{ k/in}^2) = 0.93 \text{ in}^2$$

For AW 5 × 3.70

$$A_w = dt_w = (5.00) 0.19$$

$$= 0.95 \text{ in}^2 > 0.93 \text{ in}^2$$

The AW 5 × 3.70 is therefore the lightest satisfactory beam.

**NOTES:** The building code should be checked to see if analysis for other loading conditions (such as alternate span loading) is required in addition to the load addressed above. Generally, the use of the formula $M = \pm wL^2/8$ satisfies all building code requirements for uniformly loaded beams supported on both ends.

If holes will be drilled in the flange at or near points of high tensile stress, it may be necessary to use a larger beam. This may be determined by multiplying the computed flange stress at the section under consideration by the ratio of the gross area of the flange to the net area of the flange and comparing the result with the allowable stress.

Web crippling at supports should be checked; see Example 4.

# Example 4
## SQUARE TUBE IN FLEXURE AND SHEAR
### Illustrating Sections F.2, F.3.3, G.2, and J.9.3.1

![Diagram showing a 3" square beam of Alloy 6063-T6 spanning 40 inches, simply supported at both ends, with a concentrated load of 1.50 k applied at mid-span. The beam is shown in elevation view with a square cross-section shown at left end.](3-inch square tube beam with 1.50 k load at center)

**Figure 4**

## GIVEN:

1. Concentrated load of 1.50 k including dead load, applied at mid span.
2. Span 40 in., simply supported
3. Alloy: 6063-T6
4. Product specification: ASTM B221

## FIND:

The thickness of a standard 3 in. square tube that will safely support the load, using ASD.

## SOLUTION:

From Part VI Beam Formulas Case 1, a simply supported beam with a concentrated load $P$ at the center, the maximum moment = $M = PL/4 = (1.5 \text{ k})(40 \text{ in.})/4 = 15.0 \text{ in-k}$

The available flexural strength of members is the least of the available strengths for the limit states of yielding, rupture, local buckling, and lateral-torsional buckling. Section F.1 gives a safety factor of 1.95 for the rupture limit state and 1.65 for all other limit states.

a) The allowable yield moment given in Section F.2 is $M_n/\Omega$ = $ZF_{cy}/\Omega$; using $F_{cy} = 25$ ksi (see Tables A.4.1 and A.4.3), $\Omega = 1.65$, and setting the allowable yield moment equal to the required moment:

$$ZF_{cy}/\Omega = Z(25 \text{ k/in}^3)/1.65 = 15.0 \text{ in-k}$$

gives $Z = 0.99 \text{ in}^3$.

b) The allowable moment for the limit state of rupture given in Section F.2 is $M_n/\Omega = ZF_{tu}/k_t/\Omega$; using $F_{tu} = 30$ ksi and $k_t = 1.0$ (see Table A.4.3), $\Omega = 1.95$, and setting the allowable moment equal to the required moment:

$$ZF_{tu}/k_t/\Omega = Z(30 \text{ k/in}^3)/1.0/1.95 = 15.0 \text{ in-k}$$

gives $Z = 0.98 \text{ in}^3$. Since the plastic modulus required for the yield limit state exceeds the plastic modulus required for the rupture limit state, use $Z = 0.99 \text{ in}^3$ to select a trial beam. The smallest square tube shown in Part V, Table 18 for which $Z > 0.99 \text{ in}^3$ is 3 × 3 × 0.095, for which $Z = 1.20 \text{ in}^3$, and for which

$$M_n = ZF_{cy} = (1.20 \text{ in}^3)(25 \text{ k/in}^2) = 30 \text{ in-k} < 1.5F_{cy}S =$$
$$1.5(25 \text{ k/in}^3)(1.04 \text{ in}^3) = 39 \text{ in-k}$$

c) Now check the beam for local buckling according to Section F.3.

1) The flange is in uniform compression, so its strength is given in Section B.5.4. Section B.5.4.2 addresses flat elements supported on both edges; from Part VI, Table 2-21

$$b/t = (3 - 2(0.095))/0.095 = 29.6 < 22.8 = \lambda_1 \text{ and}$$
$$< \lambda_2 = 39, \text{ so } F_c/\Omega = 19.0 - 0.170(29.6) = 14.0 \text{ ksi}$$

2) The web is in flexure, so its strength is given by Section B.5.5.1, flat elements in flexure supported on both edges. Since the beam is symmetric about the bending axis, $c_1 = -c_2$, and $m = 0.65$. Thus $\lambda_1$ equals the tabulated value of 34.7.

$b/t = (3 - 2(0.095))(0.095 = 29.6 < \lambda_1 = 34.7$,
so $F_c/\Omega = 22.7$ ksi

For a $3 \times 3 \times 0.095$ tube, with a section modulus $S = 1.04$ in³, the stress in the flange is

$$f = M/S = (15.0 \text{ in-k})(1.04 \text{ in}^3) = 14.4 \text{ ksi}$$

Using the weighted average method to determine the allowable moment for the limit state of local buckling (Section F.3.1):

$$M_{allo} = F_c I_f /c_{ef} + F_c I_w /c_{ew}$$

$$I_f = 2[d^3/12 + dt(d/2 - t/2)^2] = 1.203 \text{ in}^4$$
$$I_w = 2t(d - 2t)^3/12 = 0.351 \text{ in}^4$$

The allowable moment for local buckling is

$$M_{allo}/\Omega = (14.0 \text{ k/in}^2)(1.203 \text{ in}^4)/(3/2 - 0.095/2)\text{in.}$$
$$+ (22.7 \text{ k/in}^2)(0.351 \text{ in}^4)/(3/2 - 0.095/2)\text{in.} = 17.3 \text{ in-k}$$

The warping constant $C_w$ is approximately 0 for an angle. The torsion constant is

$$J = (1/3)(0.375)^2(4 + 3.625) = 0.134 \text{ in}^4$$

$x_o$, $y_o$ = coordinates of the shear center with respect to the centroid

$$x_o = y_o = 1.12 - 0.5(3/8) = 0.9325 \text{ in.}$$

Chapter E addresses columns. Section E.1 requires that the allowable compressive strength is the least of the limit states of member buckling, local buckling, and the interaction between member buckling and local buckling, and establishes $\Omega_c = 1.65$. Allowable stresses for 6061-T6 given in Part VI Table 2-19 are used below.

a) Member buckling is addressed in Section E.2. Section E.2.2 addresses flexural-torsional buckling for unsymmetric shapes such as an angle. The elastic buckling stress $F_e$ is the lowest root of the equation

$$(F_e - F_{ex})(F_e - F_{ey})(F_e - F_{ez}) - F_e^2(F_e - F_{ey})(x_o^2/r_o^2)^2$$
$$- F_e^2(F_e - F_{ex})(y_o^2/r_o^2)^2 = 0$$

where

$$r_o^2 = x_o^2 + y_o^2 + \frac{I_x + I_y}{A_g} = 2(0.9325)^2 + 2(4.26)/2.86 = 4.72 \text{ in}^2$$

$$F_{ex} = F_{ey} = \frac{\pi^2 E}{\left(\frac{k_x L_x}{r_x}\right)^2} = \frac{\pi^2(10,100)}{(27/1.22)^2} = 203.5 \text{ ksi}$$

$$F_{ez} = \frac{1}{A_g r_o^2}\left(GJ + \frac{\pi^2 EC_w}{(k_z L_z)^2}\right) = \frac{(3800)(0.134)}{(2.86)(4.72)} = 37.7 \text{ ksi}$$

Solving for $F_e$ by trial and error, $F_e = 35.0$ ksi.

$$\lambda = \pi\sqrt{\frac{E}{F_e}} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{10,100}{35.0}} = 53.4 < \lambda_2 = 66, \text{ so}$$

$$F_c/\Omega = 0.00047\lambda^2 - 0.232\lambda + 25.2$$

$$F_c/\Omega = 0.00047(53.4)^2 - 0.232(53.4) + 25.2 = 14.2 \text{ ksi}$$

The member buckling stress is $F_c = (14.2)(1.65) = 23.3$ ksi

The allowable axial compressive strength for member buckling is $P_n/\Omega = (F/\Omega)A_g = (14.2)(2.86) = 40.6$ k

b) Local buckling is addressed in Section E.3. Local buckling of the angle leg (a flat element with one edge supported) is addressed in Section B.5.4.1. The slenderness is

$$b/t = (4.00 - 0.375 - 0.375)/0.375 = 8.7$$

The slenderness is between $\lambda_1 = 6.7$ and $\lambda_2 = 12$, so

$$F_c/\Omega = 27.3 - 0.91(8.7) = 19.4 \text{ ksi}$$

The allowable axial compressive strength for local buckling is
$$P_n/\Omega = (F/\Omega)A_g = (19.4)(2.86) = 55.5 \text{ k}$$

c) The interaction between member buckling and local buckling is addressed in Section E.4. Elastic buckling stresses are given in Section B.5.6.

The elastic buckling stress of the leg (a flat element with one edge supported) for the slenderness of 8.7 determined in b) above is

$$F_e = \frac{\pi^2 E}{(5.0b/t)^2} = \frac{\pi^2(10,100)}{(5.0(8.7))^2} = 52.7 \text{ ksi} > 23.3 \text{ ksi} = \text{member}$$

buckling stress; therefore, the strength is not reduced by interaction between member and local buckling.

The allowable axial compressive strength is the lesser of 40.5 k and 55.5 k, which is 40.5 k.

**NOTES:** The allowable axial compressive strength of the latticed column must also be determined for full length buckling resistance in accordance with Section E.2. The allowable strength is the lesser of this strength and four times the allowable strength of the corner angle computed above, whichever is smaller.

$$M_{allo} = F_c I_f/c_{ef} + F_c I_w/c_{ew}$$

$$0.32) = 133.3 \text{ in-k}$$

b) Section F.3 addresses local buckling.

Section B.5.4.1 addresses the flange. The slenderness ratio of the compression flange is

$$b/t = (16 - 3/8)/2/1 = 7.8$$

For the unwelded portion of the flange

$$\lambda_1 = (B_p - F_{cy})(5.0D_p) = (39.0 - 27.9)(5.0(0.297)) = 7.5$$

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{k_1 B_p}{5.0D_p} = \frac{0.50(39.0)}{5.0(0.297)} = 13.1$$

$$\lambda_1 = 7.5 < b/t = 7.8 < 13.1 = \lambda_1, \text{ so}$$
$$F_{clo} = B_p - 5.0D_p b/t = 39.0 - 5.0(0.297)(7.8) = 27.4 \text{ ksi}$$

$$F_{clo}/\Omega = (27.4 \text{ ksi})/1.65 = 16.6 \text{ ksi}$$

For the welded portion of the flange

$$\lambda_1 = (B_p - F_{cy})(5.0D_p) = (25.7 - 19)(5.0(0.158)) = 8.5$$

$$b/t = 7.8 < 8.5 = \lambda_1, \text{ so}$$
$$F_{clo}/\Omega = F_{cyw}/\Omega = (19 \text{ ksi})/1.65 = 11.5 \text{ ksi}$$

Section B.5.4 provides the strength of the compression flange as

$$F_{cf} = F_{cy}(1 - A_{wz}/A_f) + F_{cz} A_{wz}/A_f$$

The gross area of the compression flange is

$$A_f = 16(1) = 16 \text{ in}^2$$

The weld-affected area of the compression flange is

$$A_{wz} = 2.375 \text{ in}^2$$

$$F_{cf}/\Omega = [F_{cy}(1 - A_{wz}/A_f) + F_{cyw} A_{wz}/A_f]/\Omega$$

$$F_{cf}/\Omega = [16.6(1 - 2.375/16) + 11.5(2.375)/16]$$
$$= 15.8 \text{ ksi}$$

Section B.5.5.1 addresses the web.

The slenderness ratio of the web is

$$b/t = (50 - 2)/0.375 = 128$$

$$c_c = -22.9 + 1 = -21.9$$

$$c_t = 27.1 - 1 = 26.1$$

$$c_t/c_c = 26.9/-21.9 = -1.19$$

$$m = 1.3(1 - c_w/c_c) = 1.3(1 - (-1.19)) = 0.592$$

For the unwelded portion of the web

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{k_1 B_w}{mD_w} = \frac{0.5(52.0)}{(0.59)(0.457)} = 96$$

$$b/t = 128 > 96 = \lambda_2, \text{ so}$$

$$F_{bw} = \frac{k_2 \sqrt{B_w E}}{mb/t} = \frac{2.04\sqrt{(52.0)(10,100)}}{(0.592)(128)} = 19.5 \text{ ksi}$$

$$F_{bw}/\Omega = 19.5/1.65 = 11.8 \text{ ksi}$$

For the welded portion of the web

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{k_1 B_w}{mD_w} = \frac{0.5(34.1)}{(0.592)(0.243)} = 118.5$$

$$b/t = 128 > 121 = \lambda_2, \text{ so}$$

$$F_{bw} = \frac{k_2 \sqrt{B_w E}}{mb/t} = \frac{2.04\sqrt{(34.1)(10,100)}}{(0.592)(128)} = 15.8 \text{ ksi}$$

$$F_{bw}/\Omega = 15.8/1.65 = 9.6 \text{ ksi}$$

Section B.5.5 provides the strength of the web in compression as

$$F_c = F_{cy}(1 - A_{wz}/A_w) + F_{bw} A_{wz}/A_w$$

The gross area of the web in compression is

$$A_w = 0.375(22.9 - 1) = 8.21 \text{ in}^2$$

The weld-affected area of the web in compression is

$$A_{wz} = (1)(0.375) = 0.375 \text{ in}^2$$

$$F_b/\Omega = [F_{by}(1 - A_{wz}/A_w) + F_{bw} A_{wz}/A_w]/\Omega$$

$$F_b/\Omega = [11.8(1 - 0.375/8.21) + 9.6(0.375)/8.21]$$
$$= 11.7 \text{ ksi}$$

Section F.3.1 provides the weighted average strength of the elements.

The moment of inertia of the flanges is

$$I_f = (12)(1)^3/12 + (16)(1)^3/12 + (16)(1)(22.9 - 0.5)^2$$
$$+ (12)(1)(27.1 - 0.5)^2 = 16,521 \text{ in}^4$$

The moment of inertia of the web is

$$I_w = (0.375)(48)^3/12 + (0.375)(48)(27.1 - 25)^2 = 3535 \text{ in}^4$$

$$M_{allo} = F_{cf}I_f/c_{ef} + F_{cw}I_w/c_{ew}$$

$$M_{allo}/\Omega = (15.8)(16,521)/(22.9 - 0.5) +$$
$$(11.7)(3535)/(22.9 - 1) = 13,540 \text{ in-k}$$

c) Section F.4 addresses lateral-torsional buckling. Section F.4.1.1 allows the bending coefficient $C_b$ to be conservatively taken as 1.

Since the compression flange is larger than the tension flange, Section F.4.2.2 does not apply. Section F.4.2.5 applies to any beam, so using it:

$$M_e = \frac{\pi^2 EI_y}{L_b^2}\left[U + \sqrt{U^2 + \frac{0.038JL_b^2}{I_y} + \frac{C_w}{I_y}}\right]$$

$U = C_1 g_0 + C_2 \beta_x/2$. Section F.4.2.5 permits $C_1$ and $C_2$ to be taken as 0.5, so

$$U = 0.5(-22.9 + 7.85) + 0.5(17.9)/2 = -3.05 \text{ in.}$$

$$M_e = \frac{(1)\pi^2(10,100)(485.5)}{(120)^2} \times$$

$$\left[-3.05 + \sqrt{(-3.05)^2 + \frac{0.038(10.2)(120)^2}{485.5} + \frac{243,400}{485.5}}\right]$$

$$= 66,470 \text{ in-k}$$

$$\lambda = \pi\sqrt{\frac{ES_y}{M_e}} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{(10100)(879)}{66,470}} = 36.3$$

For a beam with no portion weld-affected:

$$\lambda = 36.3 < 95.9 = C_c, \text{ so}$$
$$M_{allo b} = M_{nny}\left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{C_c}\right) + \frac{\pi^2 E\lambda S_y}{C_c^2}$$

$$= 24,870\left(1 - \frac{36.3}{95.9}\right) + \frac{\pi^2(10,100)(36.3)(877)}{95.9^2}$$

$$= 19,050 \text{ in-k}$$

$$F_b = M_{allo}/S_t = (19,050 \text{ in-k})(877 \text{ in}^3) = 21.7 \text{ k/in}^2$$

For a beam entirely weld-affected:

$$\lambda = 36.3 < 117.7 = C_c, \text{ so}$$

$$M_{allo b} = M_{nny}\left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{C_c}\right) + \frac{\pi^2 E\lambda S_y}{C_c^2}$$

$$= 16,930\left(1 - \frac{36.3}{117.7}\right) + \frac{\pi^2(10,100)(36.3)(879)}{117.7^2}$$

$$= 13,650 \text{ in-k}$$

$$F_b = M_{allo}/S_t = (13,650 \text{ in-k})(877 \text{ in}^3) = 15.6 \text{ k/in}^2$$

Section F.4 provides the lateral-torsional buckling strength of longitudinally welded beams as

$$M_b = M_{be}(1 - A_{wz}/A_f) + M_{bw}(A_{wz}/A_f)$$

where

$$A_{wz} = (1 + 0.375 + 1)(1) + (1)(0.375) = 2.75 \text{ in}^2$$

$$A_f = (16)(1) + (22.9/3 - 1)(0.375) = 18.5 \text{ in}^2$$

$$M_{allo} = 19,050(1 - 2.75/18.5) + 13,650(2.75/18.5)$$
$$= 18,250 \text{ in-k}$$

$$M_{allo}/\Omega = 18,250/1.65 = 11,060 \text{ in-k}$$

The lateral-torsional buckling stress $F_b = M_{allo}/S_t$

$$F_b = (18,250 \text{ in-k})/877 \text{ in}^2 = 20.8 \text{ k/in}^2$$

d) Section F.4.3 addresses interaction between local buckling and lateral-torsional buckling.

The flange's slenderness ratio is

$$b/t = (16 - 0.375)/2/1 = 7.8$$

The flange's elastic buckling stress given in Section B.5.6 is

$$F_e = \frac{\pi^2 E}{(5.0b/t)^2} = \frac{\pi^2(10,100)}{(5.0(7.8))^2} = 65.5 \text{ ksi} > 20.8 \text{ ksi}$$

Because the flange's elastic buckling stress is not less than the beam's lateral-torsional buckling stress, the beam's flexural capacity is not limited by the interaction between local buckling and lateral-torsional buckling.

The allowable moments are:

For yielding: $M_{ny}/\Omega = 14,350$ in-k
For rupture: $M_{nu}/\Omega = 19,190$ in-k
For local buckling: $M_b/\Omega = 13,540$ in-k
For lateral-torsional buckling: $M_b/\Omega = 11,060$ in-k

The least of these is 11,060 in-k from lateral-torsional buckling.

**NOTES:** If the shape of the moment diagram is known the lateral-torsional buckling strength can be determined more precisely by using the bending coefficient $C_b$ computed according to Section F.4.1.1.

### GIVEN:

1. ASD concentrated load of 5.5 k at mid-span
2. Span: 10 ft, simply supported
3. Alloy-temper: 6061-T6
4. Product specification: ASTM B429

### FIND:

Is a 6 in. schedule 40 pipe adequate in flexure for the required load?

### SOLUTION:

Section F.1 establishes safety factors of 1.95 on tensile rupture and 1.65 on all other limit states for flexure. Allowable stresses for 6061-T6 given in Part VI Table 2-19 are used below.

Section F.2 addresses the limit states of yielding and rupture. Part V Table 17 shows, for a 6 in. schedule 40 pipe:

$$D = 6.625 \text{ in, } t = 0.280 \text{ in., } S = 8.50 \text{ in}^3, Z = 11.3 \text{ in}^3,$$
$$I_x = 28.1 \text{ in}^4, J = 56.2 \text{ in}^4$$

For the limit state of yielding, the allowable moment is the lesser of

$$M_{ny}/\Omega = 1.5SF_{cy}/\Omega = 1.5(8.50 \text{ in}^3)(35 \text{ k/in}^2)/1.65$$
$$= 270.4 \text{ in-k}$$

$$M_{ny}/\Omega = ZF_{ty}/\Omega = (11.3 \text{ in}^3)(35 \text{ k/in}^2)/1.65 = 239.7 \text{ in-k}$$

The lesser of these is $M_{ny}/\Omega = 239.7$ in-k, and
$$M_{ny} = (239.7 \text{ in-k})(1.65) = 395.5 \text{ in-k}.$$

For the limit state of rupture, the allowable moment is

## WELDED CONNECTION
## Illustrating Section J.2

![Figure 27: Diagram showing a welded connection with longitudinal weld (6 in. on each side) and transverse weld (4 in.) connecting two plates. Load P applied at both ends.]

**Figure 27**

### GIVEN:

1. Welded connection as shown in Figure 27
2. Longitudinal weld length 6 in. (each side), transverse weld length 4 in.
3. Weld size: $S_w = 3/8$ in. equal leg fillet
4. Base metal: 6061-T6, 0.500 in. thick
5. Product specification: ASTM B221
6. Filler alloy: 4043

### FIND:

The allowable force $P$ on the connection.

### SOLUTION:

Section J.2 establishes the allowable strength of welded connections as $R_n/\Omega$, where $\Omega = 1.95$.

Section J.2.2.1 defines the effective throat of a fillet weld; the effective throat for an equal leg fillet is

$$S_{we} = 0.707S_w$$

$$S_{we} = 0.707(0.375 \text{ in.}) = 0.265 \text{ in.}$$

Section J.2.2.2 defines the effective length $L_{we}$. If longitudinal and transverse welds are not continuous at the corners:

$$L_{we} = 4 \text{ in. } > 4S_w = 4(0.375 \text{ in.}) = 1.5 \text{ in., so the full length}$$
of the weld is effective.

The length of the end-loaded fillet weld is
$6 \text{ in.} < 100(0.375 \text{ in.}) = 37.5 \text{ in.,}$
which does not exceed the maximum effective length prescribed in Section J.2.2.2.

Section J.2.5 establishes the nominal strength of a fillet weld $R_n$ as the lesser of the base material strength for the limit states of tensile rupture and shear rupture and the weld metal strength for the limit state of rupture as follows:

(a) For the base metal

$$R_n = F_{nBM} A_{BM}$$

where

$F_{nBM} =$ nominal stress of the base metal corresponding to its welded ultimate strength from Table A.4.3

For base metal in tension,
$$F_{nBM} = F_{tuw} = 24 \text{ ksi}$$

For base metal in shear,
$$F_{nBM} = 0.6F_{tuw} = 0.6(24 \text{ ksi}) = 14.4 \text{ ksi}$$

$A_{BMT} =$ cross-sectional area of the base metal in tension =
$(0.5 \text{ in.})(4 \text{ in.}) = 2.0 \text{ in}^2$

$A_{BMV} =$ cross-sectional area of the base metal in shear =
$(0.5 \text{ in.})2(6 \text{ in.}) = 6.0 \text{ in}^2$

$$R_n = (2.0 \text{ in}^2)(24 \text{ k/in}^2) + (6.0 \text{ in}^2)(14.4 \text{ k/in}^2) = 134.4 \text{ k}$$

(b) For the weld metal

$$R_n = F_{nw} A_{we}$$

where

$F_{nw} =$ nominal stress of the weld metal corresponding to its ultimate strength from Table A.4.6

$$F_{nw} = 0.6(0.85F_{tuw}) = (0.6)(0.85)(24 \text{ k/in}^2) = 12.2 \text{ k/in}^2$$

$A_{we} =$ effective area of the weld =
$$S_{we}L_{we} = (0.265 \text{ in.})(16 \text{ in.}) = 4.24 \text{ in}^2$$

$$R_n = F_{nw} A_{we} = (12.2 \text{ k/in}^2)(4.24 \text{ in}^2) = 51.7 \text{ k}$$

Because the strength of the 4043 filler ($F_{tuw} = 24$ ksi) matches the welded strength of the 6061 base metal ($F_{tuw} = 24$ ksi), it is not necessary to check the strength of the fusion zone of the filler metal to the base metal, as the fillet weld throat

is smaller than the fillet weld leg size, which is the width of the fusion zone. If the base metal's welded strength were less than the filler's strength, then the fusion zone's strength would need to be checked.

The lesser of the $R_n$ values is 51.7 k, so

$$P = R_n/\Omega = (51.7 \text{ k})/1.95 = 26.5 \text{ k}$$

**NOTES:** The strength of aluminum fillet welds is usually governed by the strength of the filler alloy, as in this example. Therefore, the joint strength can be increased by using a stronger filler, such as 5356. Table M.9.1 shows which fillers may be used.

### Calculations of Section Properties

**Element Properties**

| Element | $y$ | $L$ | $yL$ | $y^2L$ | $I$ |
|---------|-----|-----|------|--------|-----|
| 1 | 0.016 | 1.375 | 0.022 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| 2 | 0.500 | 1.090 | 0.545 | 0.272 | 0.085 |
| 3 | 0.984 | 5.625 | 5.535 | 5.446 | 0.000 |
| 4 | 0.500 | 1.090 | 0.545 | 0.272 | 0.085 |
| Totals | | 9.179 | 6.647 | 5.992 | 0.170 |

$c_y = \sum yL/\sum L = 6.647/9.179 = 0.724$ in., height of neutral axis

$I_x = [2(y^2L) - c_y^2\sum L + \sum I]t = [5.992 - (0.724)^2(9.179) + 0.170](0.032) = (1.349 \text{ in}^3)(0.032 \text{ in.})$

$I_x = 0.0432$ in⁴

$S_{bot} = I_x/c_y = 0.0432/0.724 = 0.0596$ in³

$S_{top} = I_x(\text{height} - c_y) = 0.0432/(1 - 0.724) = 0.1565$ in³

The moment of inertia of the flanges (elements 1 and 3) is

$I_f = [(1.375)(0.724 - 0.016)^2 + (5.625)(0.984 - 0.724)^2](0.032)$
$= 0.0342$ in⁴

The moment of inertia of the webs (elements 2 and 4) is

$I_w = [2(1.090)(0.724 - 0.5)^2 + 2(0.085)](0.032)$
$= 0.0089$ in⁴

The plastic section modulus computed by finding the plastic neutral axis such that the area above this axis equals the area below is $Z = 0.0781$ in³.

Section F.1 states that the allowable moment is the least of the allowable moments for yielding, rupture, local buckling, and lateral-torsional buckling. Lateral-torsional buckling is unlikely to govern for this shape. Section F.1 also establishes safety factors of 1.95 on tensile rupture and 1.65 on all other limit states for flexure.

By Section F.2, the allowable moment for yielding is the least of

$1.5F_{cy}S_c/\Omega$

$1.5F_{ty}S_t/\Omega$

and $ZF_{cy}/\Omega$

**For the top flange in compression,**

$1.5F_{cy}S_c/\Omega = 1.5(27)(0.1565)/1.65 = 3.84$ in-k

$1.5F_{ty}S_t/\Omega = 1.5(30)(0.0596)/1.65 = 1.63$ in-k

and $ZF_{cy}/\Omega = (0.0781)(27)/1.65 = 1.28$ in-k,
so $M_{ny}/\Omega = 1.28$ in-k.

For the limit state of rupture, the allowable moment is

$M_{nu}/\Omega = ZF_{tu}/k_t/\Omega = (0.0781)(34)/1.95 = 1.36$ in-k.

The allowable moment for the limit state of local buckling is determined using Section F.3.1.

For the top flange in compression,

a) Element 3 is in uniform compression;
$b/t = \lambda = 5.625/0.032 = 175.8$.

By Section B.5.4.2, $\lambda_2 = 41.8$, so

$$F_c/\Omega = \frac{k_2\sqrt{B_e E}}{(1.6b/t)\Omega} = \frac{2.04\sqrt{(37.6)(10,100)}}{1.6(175.8)(1.65)} = 2.7 \text{ ksi}$$

b) Elements 2 and 4 are in flexural compression;
$b/t = \lambda = 1.09/0.032 = 34.1$.

By Section B.5.5.1:

$c_y = 0.724 - 1 = - 0.276$ in., $c_u = 0.724$ in.;
since $c_u/c_y = 0.724/(- 0.276) = - 2.62 < - 1$,

$m = 1.3/(1 - c_u/c_y) = 1.3/(1 - (- 2.62)) = 0.359$ and

$\lambda_1 = (B_{ne} - 1.5F_{cy})(mD_w)$
$= (50.2 - 1.5(27))/0.359/0.433 = 62.4 > 34.1$,

so $F_b/\Omega = 1.5 F_{cy} = 1.5(27)/1.65 = 24.5$ ksi

$M_{nLB}/\Omega = (F_c/\Omega) I_f/c_{cf} + (F_b/\Omega) I_w/c_{cw}$

$M_{nLB}/\Omega = (2.7)(0.0342)/(1 - 0.724 - 0.032/2) +$
$24.5(0.0089)/(0.276 - 0.032) = 1.25$ in-k

**For the bottom flange in compression,**

a) Element 1 is in uniform compression;
$b/t = \lambda = 1.375/0.032 = 43.0$.

By Section B.5.4.2, $\lambda_2 = 41.8$, so

$$F_c/\Omega = \frac{k_2\sqrt{B_e E}}{(1.6b/t)\Omega} = \frac{2.04\sqrt{(37.6)(10,100)}}{1.6(43.0)(1.65)} = 11.1 \text{ ksi}$$

b) Elements 2 and 4 are in flexural compression;
$b/t = \lambda = 1.09/0.032 = 34.1$.

By Section B.5.5.1:

$c_c = -0.724$ in., $c_u = 0.276$ in.;
since $c_u/c_y = 0.276/(-0.724) = -0.381$, and
$-1 < -0.381 < 1$,

$m = 1.15 + c_u/2c_y = 1.15 + (-0.381/2) = 0.959$ and

$\lambda_1 = (B_{ne} - 1.5F_{cy})(mD_w) = (50.2 - 1.5(27))/0.959/0.433$
$= 23.4 < 34.1$,

$\lambda_2 = k_1 B_{ne}/(mD_w) = 0.5(50.2)/0.959/0.433 = 60.4$

so $F_b/\Omega = (B_{ne} - mD_w \lambda_1)/\Omega$
$= (50.2 - 0.959(0.433)(34.1))/1.65 = 21.8$ ksi

$M_{nLB}/\Omega = (F_c/\Omega) I_f/c_{cf} + (F_b/\Omega) I_w/c_{cw}$

$M_{nLB}/\Omega = (11.1)(0.0342)/(0.724 - 0.032/2) +$
$21.8(0.0089)/(0.724 - 0.032) = 0.82$ in-k

For the bottom flange in compression, the least of the allowable moments for yielding (1.28 in-k), rupture (1.36 in-k), and local buckling (0.82 in-k) is 0.82 in-k.

$M_{nw} = (0.82)(12\text{in./ft}/(8 \text{ in./cycle})$

1. Tapping screw of 7075-T73 aluminum, 1/4" diameter, UNC thread joining 0.06 in. thick 3003-H16 sheet on top of 0.06 in. thick 5052-H32 sheet
2. Sheet product specification: ASTM B209
3. 5/8" outside diameter flat washer under the screw head
4. The hole diameter in the top sheet is 9/32 in.
5. Distance from center of screw to edge of sheet is 1 in.

## FIND:

The allowable shear and tension forces for the connection.

## SOLUTION:

1. Allowable shear force

The allowable connection shear is determined according to Section J.5.5, which specifies a safety factor $\Omega = 3.0$ for screw connection shear.

a) Section J.5.5.1 addresses bearing. Since the edge distance $e = 0.5$ in. $= 2(1/4$ in.) $= 2D$, the allowable bearing force is $2F_{tu}Dt/\Omega$. Using $F_{tu}$ from Table A.4.3, the allowable shear for bearing is

| Sheet | Alloy | Thickness (in.) | $F_{tu}$ (ksi) | $2F_{tu}Dt/\Omega$ (k) |
|-------|-------|-----------------|----------------|------------------------|
| 1 | 3003-H16 | 0.06 | 24 | 0.24 |
| 2 | 5052-H32 | 0.06 | 31 | 0.31 |

b) Section J.5.5.2 addresses screw tilting. Since
$t_2 = 0.06 < 0.06 = t_3$, the allowable screw tilting shear is

$R_v/\Omega = 4.2(t_2/D)^{1/3}F_{u2}/\Omega$
$= 4.2(0.06^{1/3}(0.25))^{1/3}(31)/3 = 0.32$ k

c) Section J.5.5.3 addresses screw shear. The single shear strength of a 1/4" 7075-T73 machine screw is given in Part VI, Table 5-1 as 1170 lb.

$R_v/(1.25\Omega) = (1170 \text{ lb})(1.25/(3)) = 0.31$ k

The allowable shear is the least of 0.24, 0.31, 0.32, and 0.31 k, so the allowable shear is 0.24 k based on bearing on the top sheet.

2. Allowable tensile force

The allowable connection tension is determined according to Section J.5.4, which specifies a safety factor $\Omega = 3.0$ for screw connection tension.

Section J.5.4 requires that the washer outside diameter $D_w$ must equal or exceed 5/16 in.:

$D_w = 5/8$ in. $> 5/16$ in.:

a) Section J.5.4.1 addresses pull-out. The allowable pull-out force is

$R_v/\Omega = K_tDL_eFt2/\Omega$

$= (1.01)(0.25)(0.06)(23)/3 = 0.12$ k

b) Section J.5.4.2 addresses pull-over. The allowable pull-over force is:

$R_v/\Omega = C_{por} t_1F_{tu1}(D_w - D_h)/\Omega$

$= (1.0)(0.06)(24)(0.625 - 9/32)/3$

$= 0.16$ k

c) Section J.5.4.3 addresses screw tension. The tensile strength of a 1/4" diameter 7075-T73 machine screw is given in Part VI, Table 5-1 as 1940 lb.

$R_v/(1.25\Omega) = (1940 \text{ lb})/(1.25(3)) = 0.52$ k

The allowable tension is the least of 0.12, 0.16, and 0.52 k, so the allowable tension is 0.12 k based on pull-out.
