**13.1.1 Scope.** This chapter establishes minimum design criteria and testing procedures for nonstructural components, including their supports and attachments.

**13.1.2 Seismic Design Category.** For the purposes of this chapter, nonstructural components shall be assigned to the same Seismic Design Category as the structure that they occupy or to which they are attached.

**13.1.3 Component Importance Factor.** All components shall be assigned a component Importance Factor as indicated in this section.

- For components in or attached to Occupancy Category IV structures, $I_p = 1.5$
- For all other cases, $I_p = 1.0$

**EXCEPTION:** The value of the component Importance Factor, $I_p$, is permitted to be taken equal to 1.0 for components in Occupancy Category IV facilities where:
1. The component conveys, supports, or otherwise contains hazardous materials as defined in Section 1.2, and
2. The component is required to function for life-safety purposes after the design earthquake, including, but not limited to, fire protection sprinkler systems, fire hydrant systems in accordance with the provisions for nonbuilding structures supported by other structures that are designed in accordance with Chapter 15.

**13.1.4 Exemptions.** The nonstructural components listed in this section are exempt from the requirements of this chapter to the extent indicated:

1. Mechanical and electrical components in structures in Seismic Design Categories B and C with $I_p = 1.0$ need not be designed for seismic forces.
2. Components that are required to function for life-safety purposes and are intended to remain in service following the design earthquake, including, but not limited to, fire hydrant systems in accordance with the provisions for nonbuilding structures supported by other structures that are designed in accordance with Chapter 15, are permitted to be designed using the provisions of Essential Facility at Essential Section 13.6.11.
3. Components that are part of the egress system of a structure, designated as Essential Facility in accordance with Chapter 1, and to other structures and components housed in or designated as Essential Facility need not be designed to function following the design earthquake except as permitted in Essential Facility 15.

**13.1.5 Reference Documents Using Alternative Stress Design.** Where a reference document provides a basis for the design of a nonstructural component and with the equivalent lateral force procedures for the design of items covered by that document, that document shall set forth the seismic load effects in terms of the allowable stress determined in accordance with the applicable nonstructural component provisions, and no increase for alternative design is permitted. If such loads are not specified, the component displacements and displacements determined in accordance with the applicable section of this chapter shall not exceed those permitted by the referenced standard.

**13.1.6 Reference Documents Using Allowable Stress Design.** Where a reference document provides a basis for the design on the basis of allowable stresses, those allowable stresses shall be permitted to be increased where the specified load combinations of Chapter 2 are used and the seismic load effect, $E$ (as in Section 13.3.1), is not exceeded. **13.1.7 Application of Nonstructural Component Requirements.** Nonstructural components and their supports shall be designed to meet the minimum requirements of this chapter, including anchors into concrete and masonry and anchorage to steel and wood structural members.

In Section 13.3, the design earthquake forces on nonstructural components and their connections to resisting structures need to meet the seismic requirements of other chapters for nonstructural elements that are part of the seismic force-resisting system and shall be designed in accordance with the requirements of Chapter 12.

### 13.2.6 SEISMIC DEMANDS ON NONSTRUCTURAL COMPONENT CONSTRUCTION
As an alternative to the analytical requirements of Sections 13.3 through 13.6, the seismic design force, $F_p$, shall be applied to the component's center of gravity and distributed relative to the component's mass distribution. The seismic design load shall be applied horizontally to structural surfaces at the points of attachment that compose the nonstructural components.

The direction of $F_p$ shall shall be so those that produce the most critical load effects on the component. In supporting structure and load effects on structural members or systems shall be reviewed for the following two cases:

- Case 1: A combination of $F_p$ at any one horizontal direction plus $F_p$ from a perpendicular horizontal direction.
- Case 2: The combination from Case 1 rotated 90 degrees.
