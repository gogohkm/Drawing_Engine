### 5.2 DEFINITIONS

**BASE FLOOD:** The flood having a 1% chance of being equaled or exceeded in any given year (also referred to as a "100-year flood").

**BASE FLOOD ELEVATION (BFE):** The elevation of the base flood, including wave height, relative to the datum specified on a flood hazard map or otherwise approved, typically North American Vertical Datum (NAVD).

**BREAKAWAY WALL:** Any type of wall subject to flooding that is not required to provide structural support to a building and that is designed to collapse under specific lateral loading forces, such that (1) it allows the passage of floodwaters and (2) it does not damage the structure or supporting foundation system.

**COASTAL A ZONE:** An area within a special flood hazard area, landward of a V-Zone or landward of an open coast without mapped V-Zones, in which the principal source of flooding is astronomical tides, storm surges, seiches, or tsunamis, not riverine flooding, where during the base flood, the potential for breaking wave heights, measured from the top of peak water surface to the bottom of the wave trough, is greater than or equal to 1.5 ft (0.45 m).

**COASTAL HIGH HAZARD AREA:** See V-Zone or landward of an open coast without mapped V-Zones.

**DESIGN FLOOD:** The greater of the following two flood events: (1) the base flood, affecting those areas identified as special flood hazard areas on the community map and is subject flood hazard areas and (2) the flood corresponding to the area design flood hazard areas.

**DESIGN FLOOD ELEVATION:** The elevation of the "design flood," including wave height, relative to the datum specified on the flood hazard map, or otherwise.

## 5.3 DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

### 5.3.1 Design Loads

Structural systems of buildings or other structures, including the foundation and all portions of the structure located below, at, or above the design flood elevation, shall be designed, constructed, connected, and anchored to prevent flotation, collapse, and permanent lateral displacement due to hydrodynamic and static loads specified in Section 5.3.1 and other loads in accordance with Chapter 2.

### 5.3.2 Erosion and Scour

The effects of erosion and scour shall be considered in the calculation of loads on buildings and other structures, in the design of the structure and its foundation, and as required by ASCE/SEI 24 in flood zones designated by local flood hazard maps. For buildings and structures required by ASCE/SEI 24 in flood hazard areas, analysis shall include consideration of the potential for scour, riverine flow, and both present and potential future conditions, erosion, and impact from waterborne or other associated debris. When the results of such analyses indicate the potential for erosion to the anticipated ground water table.

### 5.3.3 Loads during Flooding

The total load on any surface of a structure resulting from the combined action of wind and flood, or for structures resulting from flood and for other load combinations shall be obtained by applying wind, flooding, and erosion and scour effects, whichever is required by the authority having jurisdiction:

1. Hydrostatic load specified in Chapter 12, and
2. If not applicable in combination with (2).

The effects of flood loads in combination with other loads as provided in Chapter 2.

Hydrostatic loads caused by a depth of water on surfaces of enclosed spaces below the design flood shall be calculated using procedures defined in this section. ### 5.4.4.4 Breaking Wave Loads from Obliquely Incident Waves

Breaking wave forces given by Equations (5.4-6) and (5.4-7) shall be modified in instances where waves are obliquely incident. Breaking wave forces from non-normally incident waves shall be given by

$$F_{oi} = F_y \sin^2 \alpha$$ (5.4-9)

where

$F_{oi}$ = Horizontal component of obliquely incident breaking wave force, lb/ft (kN/m);
$F_y$ = Net breaking wave force (normally incident waves) acting on a vertical surface, lb/ft (kN/m); and
$\alpha$ = Horizontal angle between the direction of wave approach and the vertical surface.

### 5.4.5 Impact Loads

Impact loads result from debris, ice, and any object transported by floodwaters striking against buildings and structures or parts thereof. Impact loads shall be determined using a rational approach as concentrated loads acting horizontally at the most critical location at or below the DFE.
