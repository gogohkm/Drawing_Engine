**26.1.1 Scope** Buildings and other structures, including the Main Wind Force Resisting System (MWFRS) and all components and cladding (C&C) thereof, shall be designed and constructed to resist wind loads determined in accordance with Chapters 26 through 31.

Specific requirements for Special Wind Regions are provided in this chapter, including the MWFRS and all C&C thereof, shall also be designed in accordance with recognized literature for such design. See Commentary Section 26.5 for list of recognized literature documenting wind effects on buildings of unusual shapes or characteristics and on other structures.

The provisions of this chapter define basic wind parameters for MWFRS and C&C design in accordance with Chapters 27, 28, 29, 30, and 31.

**26.1.2 Permitted Procedures** The design wind loads for buildings and other structures, including the MWFRS and C&C, shall be determined using one of the following procedures in accordance with the specified limitations of each procedure:

1. Simplified Procedure provided in Parts 1 through 5, as specified in Chapters 27, 28, and 29, for buildings meeting the requirements of Section 26.2;

2. Analytical Procedure provided in Parts 1 through 5, as specified in Chapter 27, for MWFRS;

3. Directional Procedure for building superstructures and other structures (Chapter 27, Part 1);

4. Envelope Procedure for buildings meeting the requirements specified in Chapter 28;

5. Directional Procedure for building superstructures and other structures meeting the requirements specified in Chapter 27;

6. Wind Tunnel Procedure specified in Chapter 31.

## 26.2 DEFINITIONS

The following definitions apply only to the provisions of Chapters 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, and 31.

**APPROVED:** Acceptable to the Authority Having Jurisdiction.

**AUTHORITY HAVING JURISDICTION:** The organization, political subdivision, office, or individual(s) charged with the authority to administer and enforce the provisions of this standard. See Commentary to Section 1.3 for additional information.

**26.1.2.1 Components and Cladding** Wind loads on C&C as defined in Section 26.2 shall be determined using one of the following procedures:

1. Analytical Procedure provided in Parts 1 through 5, as specified in Chapter 30;

2. Wind Tunnel Procedure, as specified in Chapter 31.

**26.1.3 Performance-Based Procedures** Wind design of buildings and structures in accordance with performance-based procedures permitted by the Authority Having Jurisdiction is permitted, provided that the following conditions are met:

- The performance-based procedures shall demonstrate that the structure can withstand the design wind loads in accordance with requirements specified in Chapter 1;

- The provisions of this standard shall be used, as applicable, with deviations permitted by the Authority Having Jurisdiction.

## 26.4 GENERAL

**26.4.3 Wind Pressures Acting on Opposite Faces of Each Building Surface** In the calculation of design wind loads for MWFRS and for C&C, the algebraic sum of the pressures acting on opposite faces of each building surface shall be taken into account.

## 26.7 EXPOSURE

For each wind direction considered, an exposure category that adequately reflects the characteristics of ground surface irregularities shall be determined for the site at which the building or structure is to be constructed. Account shall be taken of variations in ground surface roughness that arise from natural topography and vegetation, as well as from constructed features.

**26.7.1 Wind Direction and Sectors** For each selected wind direction at which the wind loads are to be evaluated, the exposure of the building or structure shall be determined for the two upwind sectors extending 45 degrees either side of the selected wind direction. The exposures in these two sectors shall be determined in accordance with Sections 26.7.2 and 26.7.3 and the exposure resulting in the highest wind loads shall be used to represent winds from that direction.

**26.7.2 Surface Roughness Categories** A ground surface roughness within each 45-degree sector shall be determined for a distance upwind of the site as defined in Section 26.7.3 from the categories below, for the purpose of assigning an exposure category as defined in Section 26.7.3:

**Surface Roughness B:** Urban and suburban areas, wooded areas, or other terrain with numerous closely spaced obstructions having the size of single-family dwellings or larger.

**Surface Roughness C:** Open terrain with scattered obstructions having heights generally less than 30 ft (9.1 m). This category includes flat, open country and grasslands.

**Surface Roughness D:** Flat, unobstructed areas and water surfaces. This category includes smooth mud flats, salt flats, and unbroken ice.

## 26.8 TOPOGRAPHIC EFFECTS

Wind speed-up effects at isolated hills, ridges, and escarpments that constitute abrupt changes in the general topography, located in any exposure category, shall be included in the determination of design wind loads when buildings and other structures are sited on such topographic features and conditions in Section 26.8.1 are met.

**26.8.1 Wind Speed-Up over Hills, Ridges, and Escarpments** The wind speed-up effect shall be included in the determination of design wind speeds for a site situated on a hill, ridge, or escarpment (where conditions 1, 2, and 3 below are met by multiplying velocity pressure exposure coefficient $K_z$ or $K_h$ by the topographic factor $K_{zt}$ determined from Equation (26.8-1):

**Condition 1:** Where the hill, ridge, or escarpment is isolated and unobstructed upwind by other similar topographic features of comparable height for 100 times the height of the topographic feature ($100H$) or 2 mi (3.2 km), whichever is less. This distance shall be measured horizontally from the point where the difference in ground elevation is 15 ft (4.6 m), in the upwind direction.

**Condition 2:** Where the hill, ridge, or escarpment protrudes above the height of average level of the upwind terrain features within a radius of 2 mi (3.2 km) in any quadrant by a factor of two or more.

**Condition 3:** The structure is located as indicated in Figure 26.8-1 in the upper one-half of a hill or ridge or near the crest of an escarpment.

For buildings or other structures sited on the upper half of an isolated hill, ridge, or escarpment, the design wind speed in Equation (26.10-1) shall be increased per Section 26.9.2.

**EXCEPTION:** A multiplier of 1.0 shall be used in wind-borne debris regions for Risk Category II buildings or structures, see Chapter 26, Table 1.5-1.

### 26.11 GUST EFFECTS

**26.11.1 Gust-Effect Factor:** The gust-effect factor for a rigid building or other structure is permitted to be taken as 0.85.

**Procedure:** To use this procedure, the building or other structure shall meet all of the following criteria for dynamic analysis:
1. The building height is less than or equal to 200 ft (61 m);
2. The fundamental natural frequency exceeds 1 Hz;
3. The building is located in flat terrain.

The effective height $L_{eff}$ in the direction under consideration shall satisfy the formula:

$$L_{eff} = \frac{\sum_i^n F_i}{\sum_i^n \frac{F_i}{z_i}}$$ (26.11-1)

The summations are over the height of the building, which is divided into $n$ parts to be calculated in accordance with the wind pressure as presented later. The approximate natural frequency $n_1$ of the building shall be determined by $n_1 = \text{estimated frequency}$ or

**26.11.3 Approximate Natural Frequency:** The approximate natural frequency $f_1(x)$, in Hz, of a flexible or brittle building or other structure, can be estimated by using Equations (26.11-2) and (26.11-3):

$$n_1 = 0.0365 d^{1/2}$$ (26.11-2)

For concrete and masonry buildings, the fundamental frequency may be estimated as:

$$n_1 = 13.5 N^{-0.85}$$ (26.11-2.SI)

---

For structural steel and concrete buildings with other lateral-force-resisting systems,

$$n_a = 75/h$$ (26.11-4)

$$n_a = 22.86/h$$ (26.11-4.SI)

$$n_a = 385(C_w)^{0.5}/h$$ (26.11-5)

- $h$ = Mean roof height, ft (m);

- $A_B$ = Base area of the building, ft² (m²);
- $A_i$ = Horizontal cross-sectional area of shear wall $i$, ft² (m²);
- $D_i$ = Length of shear wall $i$, ft (m); and
- $h_i$ = Height of shear wall $i$, ft (m).
