**12.1.1 Basic Requirements.** The seismic analysis and design procedures to be used in the design of building structures and their members shall be as prescribed in this section. The building structure shall include all components that are specified in the structure, or that materially influence the response of the structure to ground motion. The design ground motion values the prescribed loads of the system to resist the earthquake shaking that are classified as seismic force-resisting systems shall be selected and their configurations established to conform to the requirements of this chapter. The building structure shall be designed and constructed to resist the seismic effects as specified in this chapter. The structural system used to resist the seismic forces shall be in conformance with the requirements of this chapter. The foundation shall be designed and constructed in accordance with Chapter 12.

**12.1.2 Member Design, Connection Design, and Deformation Limit.** Structural members shall be proportioned and seismically force-resisting portions designed in accordance with the applicable portions of this standard, as further discussed in Section 12.1.3, and shall be designed to provide a complete path for force transfer using ductile elements. The design ground motions of this chapter need not be considered in combination with wind. The seismic portion of the design shall be based on the applicable requirements described in this chapter. Individual structural elements shall be designed in accordance with Table 12.2-1. The design strength of the members shall be based on the proper ties of the materials specified in the applicable material reference documents and specifications of this standard based on the forces indicated in the final design of the structure. The calculated deflections need not exceed the maximum forces that the system is capable of developing. Any increase in design force-resisting capacity of the structure and the dynamic properties of the structure shall be considered in design. The specified design forces need not exceed the maximum forces that the system is capable of developing. The calculated deflections need not exceed the maximum forces that the structure can actually develop and the dynamic response acceleration parameter, $S_{DS}$, times the weight of the structure.

**12.1.3 Continuous Load Path and Interconnection.** A continuous load path, or paths, with adequate strength and ductility shall be provided to transfer all seismic forces from the point of application to the final point of resistance. All parts of the structure between separation joints shall be interconnected to form a continuous path for the seismic forces, and connections shall be capable of transmitting the seismic forces induced by the parts being connected. Any smaller portion of the structure shall be tied to the remainder of the structure with sufficient design strength to resist either 5 percent of the weight of that portion or the portion required by Sections 12.11.2 for structural walls, whichever is greater.

## 12.2.3.2 Changes in Lateral-Force-Resisting System—Seismic Design Categories D through F

For structures assigned to Seismic Design Categories D through F, one of the following conditions shall be met to remove the irregularity:

(a) The lateral force resistance provided by the system below the discontinuity in each direction at each floor level is at least 75% of the average seismic resistance provided above and the length of the discontinuity is less than one-half of the plan dimension, measured perpendicular to the direction of discontinuity, or

(b) A special moment frame that is not abruptly discontinued shall be used to accommodate the discontinuity to the foundation level, or to a level where the irregularity no longer exists. The special moment frame shall be designed to resist at least 25% of the seismic forces in that direction or as required by load combination 8 of Section 12.2.3.1, whichever is more severe.

### 12.8.1 Seismic Base Shear

The seismic base shear, $V$, in a given direction shall be determined in accordance with the following equation:

$$V = C_s W$$ (12.8-1)

where

$C_s = $ The seismic response coefficient determined in accordance with Section 12.8.1.1; and
$W = $ The effective seismic weight per Section 12.7.2.

### *12.8.1.1 Calculation of Seismic Response Coefficient*

When using Equation (12.8-1), the seismic response coefficient, $C_s$, shall be determined in accordance with Equation (12.8-2). Where Equation (12.8-2) is used, the value of $C_s$ is independent of the height of the structure.

$$C_s = \frac{S_{DS}}{R/I_e}$$ (12.8-2)

The value of $C_s$ computed in accordance with Equation (12.8-2) need not exceed the following:

$$C_s = \frac{S_{D1}}{T(R/I_e)}$$ (12.8-3)

for $T \leq T_L$,

$$C_s = \frac{S_{D1}T_L}{T^2(R/I_e)}$$ (12.8-4)

and for $T > T_L$,

$$C_s = \frac{S_{D1}T_L}{T^2(R/I_e)}$$ (12.8-5)

where $T$ and $T_L$ are as defined in this section.

*For structures Located Where $S_1$ is Equal to or Greater than 0.6g*, $C_s$ shall not be less than:

$$C_s = 0.5S_1/(R/I_e)$$ (12.8-6)

*For All Other Structures, $C_s$ shall not be less than:*

$$C_s = 0.044S_{DS}I_e ≥ 0.01$$ (12.8-7)

In addition, for structures located where $S_1$ is equal to or greater than 0.6g and the seismic force-resisting system is comprised of moment frames of steel or concrete, the seismic response coefficient determined in accordance with Equation (12.8-6) shall apply. Where permitted to use orthogonal system or seismic force-resisting system (SFRS) as defined in Section 11.2, the value of $C_s$ need not be determined in accordance with Equation (12.8-7) or (12.8-6).

When Standard 11-0 is 2a and 16 and $\rho$ based [see (12.3-3)] then the design response modification factor used in this Standard 11-0 shall not be determined to exceed the following.

$$C_s = \frac{1.0S_1}{T(R/I_e)}$$ (12.8-8)
