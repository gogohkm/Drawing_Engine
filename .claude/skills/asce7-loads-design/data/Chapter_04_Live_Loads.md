The following definitions apply only to the provisions of this chapter.

**CIRCULATION PATH:** Any interior or exterior way of passage from one place to another within a building or site that is permanently established as a route.

**GUARD SYSTEM:** A component or assembly of components, including railings, taller than a guard system, serving as a barrier and a guard to prevent falling from elevated and unprotected sides of walkways, balconies, stairs, ramps, and other walking surfaces.

**HANDRAIL:** A rail that is used for grasping by a hand for guidance and support. The profile of handrails shall permit grasping by the hand and shall be continuous along the full length of stairways or stairways or the full length of ramps. Terminations at both ends shall not leave sharp or abrupt ends to contact.

**HELIPADS:** A structural surface that is used for landing or takeoff of helicopters or other aircraft.

**MULTIPURPOSE ASSEMBLY USE:** A building or portion of a building designed for any combination of the following that does not include construction or the building for each activity.

## 4.2 LOADS

All references to uniformly distributed live loads in this standard are to uniformly distributed loads per area. Loads are permitted to be placed in a pattern per the sections that address the loads for the most critical result of the loads on the structure or structural system.

## 4.3 UNIFORMLY DISTRIBUTED LIVE LOADS

### 4.3.1 Required Live Loads

The live loads used in the design of buildings and other structures shall be the maximum loads expected by the intended use or occupancy load, but shall in no case be less than the minimum load stipulated in accordance with Section 4.3.2.

### 4.3.2 Provision for Partitions

In office buildings and other buildings where partition locations shall be subject to change, the live load used in the design shall be not less than 15 psf (0.72 kN/m²) applied to any direction at any point on the structure.

### 4.5.1.1 Uniform Load

Handrail and guard systems shall also be designed to resist a load of 50 lb/ft (pound-force per linear foot) (0.73 kN/m) applied in any direction along the top. This load need not be assumed to act concurrently with the concentrated load specified in Section 4.5.1.

**EXCEPTIONS:** The uniform load need not be considered for the following circumstances:

1. One- and two-family dwellings;
2. Factory, industrial, and storage occupancies in areas that are not accessible to the public and that serve an occupant load not greater than 50; and
3. Roofs not intended for occupancy.

### 4.5.1.2 Guard System Component Loads

Balusters, panel fillers, and guard system infill components, including all rails except the top rail, shall be designed to resist a single concentrated horizontally applied normal load of 50 lb (0.22 kN) on an area not to exceed 12 in. × 12 in. (305 mm × 305 mm), including openings and space between rails and located so as to produce the maximum load effect. Reactions due to this loading are not required to be superimposed with the loads specified in Sections 4.5.1 and 4.5.1.1.

Grab bar systems and shower seats shall be designed to resist a single concentrated load of 250 lb (1.11 kN) applied in any direction at any point on the grab bar or seat to produce the maximum load effect.

## 4.8 REDUCTION OF LIVE LOADS

### 4.8.1 General

The minimum uniformly distributed live loads specified in Table 4.3-1 are permitted to be reduced in accordance with Section 4.8.2 for members with an influence area exceeding 400 ft² (37 m²) as specified by Equation (4.8-1) except for the following:

### 4.8.2 Ordinary Roofs, Awnings, and Canopies

Ordinary flat roofs, pitched roofs, and curved roofs subject to the live loads specified in Section 4.9 shall be designed for a reduced roof live load in accordance with Section 4.9 or a uniform design live load of $R_1R_2L_r$ not to exceed 400 psf (37.2 m²). For ordinary roofs with a slope less than 4 inches per foot of horizontal run or less than 4 on 12 (18.4°), a live load of 20 psf (0.96 kN/m²) shall be used for other controlling combinations with other loads except as described in Section 4.9.

### 4.8.3 Occupiable Roofs

Roofs that for which the live load is less than 100 psf (4.79 kN/m²), as specified in Section 4.3, shall be designed for a reduced live load in accordance with the applicable requirements in Section 4.7.3. Roofs for which the live load is 100 psf (4.79 kN/m²) or greater shall be designed for the unreduced live load in accordance with Section 4.3.

### 4.8.4 Heavy Live Loads

Live loads that exceed 100 psf (4.79 kN/m²) shall not be reduced except for members supporting two or more floors. For members supporting two or more floors, live loads on columns, beams, and other supporting elements shall be permitted to be reduced in accordance with Section 4.7.3 as modified according to Equation (4.8-1).

### 4.8.5 Parking Structure Design Loads

For parking garages, live loads shall be permitted to be reduced in accordance with Section 4.9, and reduction of live load is permitted in accordance with Section 4.7.2 for use in design of members supporting parking areas.

**EXCEPTIONS:** The vehicular live load per Section 4.10 must be reduced. Sections 4.7.2 and 4.8.3 shall not apply to vehicular live loads.

## 4.9 ALTERNATIVE UNIFORM FLOOR LIVE LOADS

As an alternative to the minimum loads in Section 4.3, floor live loads are permitted to be determined in accordance with approved probabilistic methods, such as those promulgated by state or local building officials, or as specified in Section 4.9.1 or 4.9.2 according to the applicable use.

## 4.10 PASSENGER VEHICLE GARAGES

### 4.10.1 Passenger Vehicle Garages

Floors in garages and driveways for passenger vehicles with no more than nine passenger seats, with or without drivers, shall be designed to support the uniformly distributed live load of Table 4.3-1 in accordance with Section 4.3.1. In addition to the uniform live load, floors in such parking garages and driveways shall be designed to support a concentrated load of 3,000 lb (13.35 kN) applied over a 20 in. × 20 in. (500 mm × 500 mm) area located to produce the maximum load effects.

### 4.11 HELIPAD LOADS

### 4.11.1 General

Helipads to be used for helicopters up to the maximum anticipated weight shall be designed. The design load shall be not less than 50% of the maximum anticipated gross weight of the helicopter or takeoff weight of the anticipated helipad uses. General information regarding helicopters and takeoff locations, considering the helicopter's fuel loading and landing area requirements, shall be provided by the helipad operator or shall be assumed by the designer.

### 4.11.2 Unidentifiable Attics

Storage loads specified in Table 4.3-1 shall not be reduced.

**EXCEPTION:** Where takeoff and landing information may be used to generate more accurate loads and for helicopters with specific site(s) use of the site, such loads are permitted to be used for design.

1. A uniform live load of 12 psf (0.58 kN/m²)
2. A concentrated load in accordance with the provisions of Section 4.9

The specified roof live load in Section 4.9 shall be permitted to be reduced 50 percent (0.57 kN/m²).
