Dead load consists of the weight of all materials permanently installed in walls, floors, roofs, ceilings, stairways, built-in partitions, finishes, cladding, and other similarly incorporated architectural and structural items and fixed service equipment, including the weight of cranes.

### 3.1.2 Weights of Materials of Construction

In determining dead loads for purposes of design, the weight of fixed service equipment, including the structural elements and other structural elements, and minimum design dead loads shall be computed considering both fully saturated and unit storage dry materials, and drainage layer materials, where applicable. Unit weights of materials and constructions provided by the manufacturer or suppliers shall be used where available. Where such information is not available, values approved by the Authority Having Jurisdiction shall be used in lieu of values provided in Table 3.2-1.

### 3.1.3 Weight of Fixed Service Equipment

The weight of fixed service equipment, such as plumbing stacks and risers, electrical feeders, heating, ventilating, and air conditioning systems, fire sprinkler systems, and other fixed service equipment, shall be considered in determining dead load in accordance with this standard. Where applicable, a liquid load or lateral liquid thrust shall be computed considering both fully saturated soil and drainage layer materials, and net results of hydrostatic uplift or uplift loads arising from underground construction or water level shall be considered in determining dead load effects.

### 3.1.4 Vegetative and Landscaped Roofs

The weight of all landscaping and hardscaping materials, including vegetative, and landscaped roofs shall be computed considering both fully saturated soil and drainage layer materials, and net results from such loadings shall be considered in determining dead load effects. Section 4.9.1 for roof live load is not applicable to vegetative and landscaped roofs, and minimum design dead loads shall be used for vegetative and landscaped roofs.

### 3.1.5 Solar Panels

The weight of solar panels, their support systems, and ballast shall be considered dead loads.

### 3.2.2 Uplift Loads on Floors and Foundation Elements

Floors, slabs on ground, foundations, and similar approximately horizontal features shall be designed for uplift from soil and/or water at a hydrostatic load shall be determined based on the elevation of the water level where the design water level is not known, the elevation of the water level shall be taken as either where the site and soil conditions indicate a higher water level is applicable or as determined by a geotechnical investigation.
