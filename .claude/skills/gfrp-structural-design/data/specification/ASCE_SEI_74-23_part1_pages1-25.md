# PREFACE

This standard is intended for use in the design of new buildings and other structures that use standard shapes from pultruded fiber reinforced polymer (FRP) composite structural shapes, connections, and assemblies. The need for this standard does not cover all aspects of FRP pultruded structural design; it supplements general building codes in the areas where the material is nonmetallic and where the mechanical resistance and other architectural conditions with a polymeric composite structural system differ from the methods and assumptions presented in contemporary design standards for metals. The latest edition of the Standards Activities Division of the Structural Engineering Institute of ASCE has produced ASCE/SEI 7.

The following principles developed in this standard, to address engineering properties and performance through multiple load provisions, were developed to apply to buildings and other structures that use FRP pultruded structural shapes. These structural shapes must have a minimum of specified mechanical property values that define minimum design properties for these materials. This standard provides a framework within which structural systems, members, and components may be lightly designed and operated with a reasonable degree of safety, and consistent with contemporary criteria for metals and other materials systems, whether structural or non-structural. These shapes may be useful in their service environment under consideration.

**1.3 MATERIALS**

This section provides general requirements for FRP composite materials and composite elements related to design for service environments as specified. Special attention shall be given to:

1. Temperature range of application including the high temperature limits. Service exposure over long-term exposure to the environmental temperature is an application criterion.
2. Presence of moisture, including moisture vapor.
3. Contact of corrosive or materials that may react with resins.
4. Ultraviolet light exposure during service.

**1.3.1 FRP Constituent Materials.** Pultruded materials shall conform to the requirements of ASTM D6662.

Glass Fibers: All rovings are composed of E-glass fibers. The basic continuous strand glass fibers in the glass roving are well distributed within the pultruded shape and well wetted by the polymer (resin), matrix-glass fiber bond strength. E-glass fibers for fiberglass reinforcements typically have a strength range from 350,000 psi (2.45 GPa) to 500,000 psi (3.45 GPa).

**Fiber Type:** Continuous lengths of E-glass fibers or glass fiber reinforcement shall have resin coating and surfacing. Chopped glass strands should be embedded in a random orientation within the laminate. Continuous strands generally create layup and properties in preferred directions. Continuous strand mats, continuous filament mats, and chopped fiber reinforcements are acceptable. Thermosetting resins, polyester resins, continuous filament mats, all of which must meet the requirements of ASTM D6662.

Resin-Matrix: The polymer matrix consists of polymers having intermediate carbon-carbon bonds and containing resins for pultruded FRP structural components. Thermoset resin systems include either polyester, vinyl ester, or epoxy resin. Thermosetting resins define the mechanical performance of FRP laminates or cross-sectional shape and durability. Both polymer types must withstand the service loads and environmental conditions.
